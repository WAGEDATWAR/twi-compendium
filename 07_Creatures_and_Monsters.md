## Shield Spiders
- A Shield Spider is a huge spider whose silver-and-black plated carapace covers its legs and body like armour. (Book 1, Ch1.29 L158-160)
- A large Shield Spider stands nearly half as tall as a grown woman. (Book 1, Ch1.29 L176)
- A Shield Spider clicks its mandibles as it approaches prey. (Book 1, Ch1.29 L161-162)
- Shield Spiders lair in large underground nests full of egg sacs, covered by a pit trap of false ground made of dead grass and sticks. (Book 1, Ch1.29 L164-166)
- One large spider will hunt a single target steadily while the rest of the nest keeps swarming around its eggs. (Book 1, Ch1.29 L168-169)
- {belief} A Silver-rank adventurer says Shield Spiders are notorious for tracking and killing prey across thousands of leagues. (Book 1, Ch1.29 L251)
- A thrown rock does not crack a Shield Spider's armour. (Book 1, Ch1.29 L175)
- Acid pits a Shield Spider's armour but does not stop it, and Rock Crabs and Shield Spiders are described as mostly immune to acid flasks. (Book 1, Ch1.29 L182-183; Book 1, Ch1.32 L160)
- Shield Spiders have a decently strong venom. (Book 1, Ch1.29 L240)
- {belief} A single Shield Spider is called a Silver-rank threat, and a nest might bring out a team of Gold-rank adventurers. (Book 1, Ch1.29 L243)
- {belief} Shield Spiders cannot be tamed unless the tamer has the [Beastmaster] class. (Book 1, Ch1.29 L306)
- Shield Spiders are weak against fire, and a nest can be burned with [Fireball] spells and shield-bearers. (Book 1, Ch1.00H L247)
- Shield Spider carcasses do not burn up in flames, so they can still be claimed for the bounty. (Book 1, Ch1.29 L325)
- The Adventurer's Guild pays an average of eleven pieces of silver per Shield Spider slain and pays more for the bodies. (Book 1, Ch1.30 L59)
- A [Blacksmith] with the right skills can make shields and armour from Shield Spider bodies. (Book 1, Ch1.30 L57)
- Most adventurers who are not Silver-rank or higher would rather run than fight a Shield Spider. (Book 1, Ch1.30 L64)
- Erin Solstice earned just over eleven gold coins from the bounty and the carcasses of the nest she killed, less because some were burned too badly to use. (Book 1, Ch1.32 L21)
- Shield Spiders normally come in nests of at least fifty and as many as three hundred. (Book 3, Ch3.22L L148)
- Antinium Soldiers can crush a Shield Spider nest by leaping into the pit and stamping the spiders and eggs to death. (Book 3, Ch3.22L L139-141)
- Shield Spider nests are pit-trap lairs that lure in prey as food for the winter. (Book 3, Ch3.22L L132; Book 3, Ch3.22L L197)
- Shield Spiders swarm up from a collapsed pit to bite at intruders. (Book 2, Ch2.11 L246-248)
- Shield Spiders bite at a skeleton and web it up until they realize there is nothing to bite. (Book 2, Ch2.00T L81)
- The Redfang Goblin tribe wears armour made from Shield Spiders and has even managed to tame them. (Book 2, Ch2.00G L162)
- A Goblin commander rides a vast Shield Spider into battle against Humans at Esthelm. (Book 3, Ch3.19T L269; Book 3, Ch3.20T L185)
- A Shield Spider ridden by a Goblin commander is killed in one blow by a stronger attacker. (Book 3, Ch3.20T L275)
- A market seller advertises a Shield Spider trained by an expert [Beast Master] as invaluable protection. (Book 4, Ch3.35 L209)
- Shield Spiders in a deep nest can be bigger than Rock Crabs. (Book 5, Ch4.08T L110)
- Crelers tear Shield Spiders apart, since the spiders' bites do only superficial injuries. (Book 7, Ch5.06M L282-283)
- A dungeon room hides a mile-deep pit of Shield Spiders, some the size of a house. (Book 8, Ch5.28 L542-544)

## Rock Crabs
- A Rock Crab hides under a boulder-like hollow shell as tall as two people and as long, and lifts the shell to scuttle on countless legs. (Book 1, Ch1.02 L63-67)
- Its pincers are long and dark brownish orange, and it makes a loud clicking like gunshots as it runs. (Book 1, Ch1.02 L65-67; Book 1, Ch1.02 L70)
- A Rock Crab gives up a chase after a while and wanders back across the plains. (Book 1, Ch1.02 L72)
- Rock Crabs move in herds, and the slimy seed cores of the blue fruit make them retreat. (Book 1, Ch1.08 L37)
- Rock Crabs and Shield Spiders are mostly immune to acid. (Book 1, Ch1.32 L160)
- Erin Solstice believes a Rock Crab could level her inn. (Book 1, Ch1.32 L161)
- A wild Rock Crab is slow-moving and ignores people who leave it alone. (Book 1, Ch1.33 L74-80)
- Goblins kill a Rock Crab by swarming it, clinging to its shell and crawling underneath, and eat its guts and bitter flesh. (Book 1, Ch1.23 L30-41)
- A Rock Crab has pathetic eyesight, so Rags's Goblins kill one from inside its shell. (Book 2, Ch2.00G L13-19)
- It normally takes around twenty Goblin lives to bring down one Rock Crab, but the meat feeds the tribe for about a week. (Book 2, Ch2.00G L28; Book 2, Ch2.00G L34)
- A Rock Crab is near-certain death for one Goblin or a small party, but only many deaths for a tribe. (Book 2, Ch2.00G L61)
- Rock Crabs look like snowy mounds in winter and can be camouflaged by snow until they strike. (Book 2, Ch2.01G L75; Book 4, Ch3.38 L374)
- Sending a melee group against a Rock Crab leaves red smears unless the adventurers are very high level. (Book 2, Ch2.22 L4)
- The Rock Crab is an apex predator of the plains, and it preys on Corusdeer, sheep and goats. (Book 3, Ch3.22L L184-185)
- A Rock Crab avoids large Shield Spider nests and hides in spring from predators that snack on it. (Book 3, Ch3.22L L186)
- A large herd of Corusdeer makes Rock Crabs no threat. (Book 3, Ch3.22L L194)
- Liscor's Watch advises that Rock Crabs be engaged with caution if at all. (Book 3, Ch3.23L L122)
- Rock Crabs are still seen on the Floodplains of Liscor in Book 7. (Book 7, Ch5.07 L338)

## Corusdeer
- A Corusdeer is an oversized reindeer with orange-brown antlers and sooty marks on its fur. (Book 2, Ch2.01T L66)
- A Corusdeer's horns glow until they are nearly white with heat, and it charges with an explosion of fire that makes it much faster. (Book 2, Ch2.01T L72-74)
- A wounded Corusdeer that runs out of energy stops glowing and becomes just a tired reindeer. (Book 2, Ch2.01T L104-105)
- Corusdeer and Snow Golems are natural enemies. (Book 2, Ch2.01T L95)
- Corusdeer graze in herds, and a herd's glowing horns turn an incoming wave of snow into steam. (Book 2, Ch2.01T L159-161)
- A Corusdeer herd can incinerate even the most dangerous enemy in seconds, so Rock Crabs are no threat to a large herd. (Book 3, Ch3.22L L194)
- Liscor's Watch orders Soldiers not to engage Corusdeer herds, since the herds are formidable if roused. (Book 3, Ch3.23L L122)
- Driving a Corusdeer herd away from Liscor is a public boon, especially to travellers on the northern road. (Book 3, Ch3.23L L140)
- Corusdeer meat is the local specialty near Liscor, served as ragu and as steak. (Book 5, Ch4.09 L13; Book 5, Ch4.09 L57)
- Corusdeer soup made by Erin stays warm to the touch and is magical. (Book 3, Ch3.09 L108)
- Another cook who copies Erin's Corusdeer soup exactly cannot make it work. (Book 3, Ch3.15 L152)
- An alchemist makes blaze bombs from Ignition Salamander scales and Corusdeer horns. (Book 4, Ch3.39 L260)
- An alchemist blends a bit of Corusdeer horn with sulfur to make a new explosive. (Book 4, Ch3.41 L245)
- Corusdeer horns once crushed the bones of the skeleton Toren, though they regrew. (Book 3, Ch3.17T L36)

## Crelers
- A hatchling Creler has long spider-like legs with barbs, a squat elongated body, a red and purple carapace with exposed organs, and a maw of circular teeth. (Book 3, Ch3.02H L437)
- Hatchling Crelers resist swords because their chitin is hard, so blunt weapons such as a mace work best. (Book 3, Ch3.02H L441-447)
- In the larval stage a Creler looks like a fat, red, faintly glowing caterpillar and is far weaker, until it turns itself inside out into its attack form. (Book 3, Ch3.02H L500-506)
- Larval Crelers left alone for a few months with food may become adults, which even Gold-rank teams do not want to fight. (Book 3, Ch3.02H L500)
- An adult Creler is far larger than a hatchling and can fly, and it takes many Soldiers to bring one down. (Book 5, Ch4.27H L440)
- Crelers are not a valid food source, because their eggs may incubate in the body of anyone who eats them. (Book 5, Ch4.27H L442-444)
- The Adventurer's Guild pays a large bounty on Crelers, and an adult carries a two thousand gold coin prize. (Book 5, Ch4.27H L431)
- Crelers lay orange and red eggs in the bodies of their dead victims. (Book 7, Ch5.06M L309)
- Crelers have an orange glowing body inside black and bloody red chitin, and their toxins poison those they wound. (Book 7, Ch5.06M L277; Book 7, Ch5.06M L289)
- Crelers are viewed as a threat to all species, created rather than born of this world, and animals ally against them. (Book 7, Ch5.06M L294-295; Book 7, Ch5.06M L305)
- {belief} Falene says even non-violent animals attack Crelers and a nest systematically wipes out all life in an area before colonizing a new location. (Book 7, Ch5.07 L175)
- Hatchlings and baby Crelers can be managed by Silver or Bronze-rank teams if the nest is small, larger nests and juveniles match Silver-rank teams, and adults are a threat for any Gold-rank team. (Book 7, Ch5.07 L178)
- In Baleros, three Gold-rank teams were once sent together as suppression teams against really big Creler nests. (Book 7, Ch5.07 L179)
- Crelers are described as a product of the blight that afflicts Rhir, and for eight hundred years the Blighted Kingdom fought the Creler Wars. (Book 14, Ch6.68 L16-18)
- During the Creler Wars over eight hundred Silver-rank teams killed Adult Crelers, and the first Blighted King was one of the individuals who passed Level 80. (Book 14, Ch6.68 L20)
- An Adult Creler speaks without words into the mind of its prey, in the same language as Skinner, and gloats at Yvlon. (Book 14, Ch6.66H L2-5)
- Slayers of an Adult Creler are given the title Hell's Wardens. (Book 14, Ch6.67 L71)

## Face-Eater Moths
- Face-Eater Moths are large insects housed in the dungeon under Liscor, so big that one has to crawl down a tunnel. (Book 6, Ch4.40L L32)
- A dungeon Face-Eater Moth can rip off a Soldier's arm and bury its mandibles in another victim. (Book 6, Ch4.40L L32)
- Antinium commanders judge that a powerful monster such as a giant Face-Eater Moth or a Crypt Worm would quickly kill regular Soldiers. (Book 6, Ch4.40L L44)
- Bird counts Face-Eater Moths among the things that are not birds and are threats, with Rock Crabs and Shield Spiders. (Book 6, Ch4.33 L284)
- The adult moth is furry with a long body, huge wings with a staring pattern of black spots on brown chitin, and a razor-sharp mouth. (Book 7, Ch5.07 L377-378)
- Thousands of smaller carnivorous moths cling to a giant moth and fly up in a swarm that covers the sun. (Book 7, Ch5.07 L378-380)
- A giant Face-Eater Moth is larger than Erin Solstice's inn, and one wingbeat sounds like thunder. (Book 7, Ch5.07 L380)
- A giant Face-Eater Moth bursts out of a rift in the Floodplains in a geyser of water and dries its wings before the swarm takes flight. (Book 7, Ch5.07 L375-377; Book 7, Ch5.07 L386)
- A report from Salazsar describes a swarm of Face-Eater Moths with individuals exceeding forty feet in height. (Book 7, Ch5.08 L248)
- The exceptionally large moths are brood mothers that lay thousands, even tens of thousands, of eggs. (Book 7, Ch5.08 L563)
- Tens of thousands of Face-Eater Moths flood the walls of Liscor and claw at the city's magic barrier. (Book 7, Ch5.07 L445-450)
- The moths cover the sky above the Floodplains and crawl over windows of the inn. (Book 7, Ch5.08 L2; Book 7, Ch5.08 L11)
- A swarm can be killed with arrows, and the moths fall wriggling when hit. (Book 7, Ch5.07 L416)
- Klbkch warns that insectile monsters survive eradication by letting their young infest new sites, so a city must clean up bodies after battle. (Book 7, Ch5.12 L449)
- Klbkch says the water will eradicate the young outside the walls, as will local wildlife. (Book 7, Ch5.12 L452)
- Some Face-Eater Moths flee Liscor and form a colony in the slopes by the Bloodfields. (Book 14, Ch6.62L L553-554)
- Three adventurer teams clear the Bloodfields moth nest with spells and arrows. (Book 14, Ch6.62L L599-602)
- The adventurers insist on full extermination because Face-Eater Moths can dig. (Book 14, Ch6.62L L610)

## Eater Goats
- Eater Goats are monsters of the High Passes, a match for a Carn Wolf, deadly, quick and fearless, and they will eat anything. (Book 6, Ch4.33 L199)
- An Eater Goat's jaws can crunch stone or metal, and the goats hunt in packs. (Book 6, Ch4.33 L199)
- They are considered a terrible threat worthy of a Gold-rank team. (Book 6, Ch4.33 L204)
- An Eater Goat bites and does not let go until it has eaten its target or died. (Book 6, Ch4.34 L7)
- An Eater Goat can stay on its feet with eight arrows in its side. (Book 6, Ch4.34 L43)
- An Eater Goat bites a thrown fire vial in mid-air and explodes, scattering the herd. (Book 6, Ch4.34 L70-71)
- Eater Goats are bigger than normal goats, with wide yellow eyes and jaws that rend steel. (Book 16, Ch7.12G L67-68)
- Eater Goats refuse to die, multiply with supernatural speed the more they eat, and charge into a wall of pikes to eat. (Book 16, Ch7.12G L68)
- Eater Goats are low on the food hierarchy of the High Passes, since Gargoyles and Wyverns can prey on them. (Book 16, Ch7.12G L69)
- Eater Goats scream like a chorus of the damned and ram a village gate with their curved horns. (Book 6, Ch4.33 L378-379)
- Redfang Goblins who paint their faces and bodies red are treated as not-Food by the Eater Goats. (Book 6, Ch4.33 L205-206)
- The Redfang Goblins follow the goats south for protection against Shield Spiders, Mothbears, Armored Crawlers and Wyverns. (Book 6, Ch4.33 L206)
- The goats rest after a feast until their systems work through the meal. (Book 6, Ch4.33 L209)
- Thousands of Eater Goats charge a Human force from the side and ignore spells and arrows, and horses do not do well against them. (Book 9, Ch5.55G L186)
- About a hundred Eater Goats gather behind a hill to attack Goblins in a valley. (Book 16, Ch7.12G L70)

## Wyverns
- Wyvern meat is a delicacy. (Book 1, Ch1.03R L5)
- Wyverns are monsters of the mountains, and white ones come down in winter because they love the cold. (Book 2, Ch2.17 L246)
- A Wyvern can dive incredibly fast, so travellers who see something overhead should find shelter. (Book 2, Ch2.17 L254)
- A Wyvern is a humongous beast, nearly three times as tall as Ryoka Griffin, a lesser Dragon without claws and with dark purple scales. (Book 2, Ch2.23 L85)
- A Wyvern can grab even an adult in seconds. (Book 2, Ch2.28 L309)
- Frost Faeries can freeze a Wyvern in seconds. (Book 2, Ch2.23 L222)
- Frost Wyverns number nearly a thousand in the High Passes under a Wyvern Lord, and he is larger than the rest. (Book 15, Ch7.01 L4-5)
- The average adult Wyvern is thirty to sixty feet from head to its long serpentine tail. (Book 15, Ch7.01 L7)
- Wyverns hate bugs, and the Wyvern Lord passes Liscor because it is too small. (Book 15, Ch7.01 L6-8)
- A Dragon drove the Wyverns from their challenge to his territory in the High Passes. (Book 15, Ch7.01 L5)
- The Wyvern Lord senses magic and a Dragon-like aura from Pallass, and few monsters stray near it. (Book 15, Ch7.02 L629-631)
- The Wyvern Lord turns on Pallass to claim a nest. (Book 15, Ch7.02 L632)
- A Greater Wyvern is practically impossible to kill with magic, and no one below Level 30 will tackle it. (Book 15, Ch7.03 L1136)
- Massed Skills throw the Wyvern Lord back when Gold-rank adventurers and elite Soldiers charge it. (Book 15, Ch7.03 L1140)

## Ashfire Bees

- Ashfire Bee honey is a poor reagent, but it could act as a potent base for potions. (Book 2, Ch2.37 L368)
- {belief} Octavia says Ashfire Bees are considered a Gold-rank threat and practically impossible to kill without mages. (Book 2, Ch2.36 L27)
- A bee hive in a cave is not calmed by smoke, because Ashfire Bees scatter and extinguish the fire instead. (Book 3, Ch3.21L L279-281; Book 10, Ch6.08 L637)
- Ashfire Bees can patrol twenty miles around their hive and return together when the hive is in danger. (Book 10, Ch6.08 L646-647)
- Ashfire Bees fly at thirty miles per hour, faster than a honeybee or a hornet. (Book 10, Ch6.08 L655-656)
- Ashfire Bees follow the instinct of finding an intruder and stinging it, and a hive is led by a queen with workers and drones. (Book 10, Ch6.08 L665; Book 10, Ch6.08 L760)
- The Adventurer's Guild ranks an Ashfire Bee as a Silver-rank threat, but hives are far more dangerous than lone bees. (Book 10, Ch6.16 L49; Book 10, Ch6.16 L55)
- Two Gold-rank teams and the Horns of Hammerad go to take on an Ashfire Bee nest for the Watch Captain. (Book 10, Ch6.16 L44; Book 10, Ch6.16 L53)
- Apista is an Ashfire Bee queen twice the size of Ryoka Griffin's hand, kept as a pet by Lyonette du Marquin, who has a lesser bond with her. (Book 5, Ch4.24 L30-31)
- Apista can burst into flames and jet fire from her body, and she scares off a larger swarm and its queen. (Book 10, Ch6.08 L680-682)
- Apista claims the hive's territory without killing the queen. (Book 10, Ch6.08 L759-760)

## Acid Flies
- An Acid Fly is a beetle-like insect with four legs, a black body and a glowing green abdomen. (Book 1, Ch1.06 L29; Book 1, Ch1.06 L34-35)
- Flicking an Acid Fly makes its abdomen burst in a splash of acid that burns skin. (Book 1, Ch1.06 L36-40)
- Only the male Acid Flies carry the acid, which they use to dissolve dead flesh into a syrup for themselves and the females. (Book 2, Ch2.04 L122-124)
- Acid Flies are a local creature near Liscor, and Drakes and Antinium eat them. (Book 1, Ch1.37 L151-153)
- Acid Fly juice sold as flasks costs twelve silver coins each and kills a Ghoul in under a minute. (Book 11, Ch6.32 L862-868)
- Acid Fly juice melts organic material in seconds but not glass or metal. (Book 11, Ch6.32 L868)
- Rock Crabs and Shield Spiders are mostly immune to acid flasks. (Book 1, Ch1.32 L160)

- A Razorbeak's egg is about three times the size of a normal bird egg. (Book 1, Ch1.06 L142)
- Razorbeaks have knife-sharp teeth and peck and dive at those who steal their eggs. (Book 1, Ch1.06R L134; Book 1, Ch1.08 L29-31)
- Razorbeaks come to feed on carcasses and entrails in a fight and bite at those nearby. (Book 1, Ch1.08R L181)
- A Razorbeak tries to lift Mrsha, a Gnoll child, and carry her away. (Book 5, Ch4.24 L153-154)
- A Razorbeak is killed with a thrown kitchen knife. (Book 5, Ch4.24 L160-161)
- Bird shoots eight Razorbeaks with arrows in one hour. (Book 7, Ch5.06M L329-331)
- Razorbeaks are seen in the air over Liscor's Floodplains. (Book 7, Ch5.07 L338)

## Carn Wolves
- Carn Wolves are giant rust-red wolves of the High Passes that like to sneak up and kill Goblins. (Book 2, Ch2.12 L205; Book 2, Ch2.01G L79)
- Ryoka Griffin first judges a Carn Wolf to be only about twice as big as a normal wolf, with rust-red fur and visible muscles. (Book 1, Ch1.08R L74-76)
- Rags judges Carn Wolves to be at least three times as large as a regular wolf and able to bring down a horse in a single strike. (Book 2, Ch2.02G L63)
- Carn Wolves are deadly enemies to Goblins, but the Red Fang Tribe rides them as mounts. (Book 2, Ch2.02G L63; Book 2, Ch2.02G L67)
- A Red Fang rider's Carn Wolf crunches a Goblin in an instant, and the mounts do not flinch at a crowd of Goblins. (Book 2, Ch2.02G L87; Book 2, Ch2.02G L104)
- A pack of Carn Wolves hesitates when many smaller enemies face it. (Book 2, Ch2.01G L84)
- Eater Goats can tear Carn Wolves apart. (Book 1, Ch1.11R L157)
- Bear traps are said to be useless on Gargoyles and Eater Goats and maybe useful on Carn Wolves. (Book 8, Ch5.38 L498)
- Feral Carn Wolves, Eater Goats and Gargoyles clash with the Raskghar at the base of the mountains. (Book 8, Ch5.41 L195)

- Redfang Goblins tie an Eater Goat to a pole to lure Gargoyles. (Book 8, Ch5.36 L248)

## Stone Starers

- A Stone Starer has long wiry arms of the same stone shell and shoots beams from the white hole in its front. (Book 8, Ch5.40 L210-211)
- The beams pierce weak enchanted metal and steel with ease, so attacking from the front is dangerous. (Book 8, Ch5.40 L211)
- Stone Starers are midriff-high, and a pickaxe blow to the head kills one and spills grey-blue blood. (Book 18, Ch7.33I L435)
- Stone Starers can be evaded, unlike Crelers or Rockmites, and their magical eyes are a product of magical gemstones. (Book 18, Ch7.33I L432-433)
- A Stone Starer nest makes the Miners pull back and call in adventurer teams. (Book 18, Ch7.33I L428-431)

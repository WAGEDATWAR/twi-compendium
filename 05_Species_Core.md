# Core Species: Humans, Drakes, Gnolls, Antinium, Goblins, Raskghar
> Scope: the six core peoples of southern Izril and their neighbours (biology, society, history, relations, change over books); every other sentient species is in 06_Species_Other.md.
> Keys: Humans, Earthers, Drakes, Gnolls, Antinium, Goblins, Raskghar
> See also: 06_Species_Other.md, 07_Creatures_and_Monsters.md, 04_Cosmology_Gods_Names_Dungeons.md

## Humans
- Humans live across the entire northern part of the continent, up to where the mountain range starts, according to Selys's map. (Book 1, Ch1.28 L225-226)
- Selys says most adventurers at the Liscor guild are Humans. (Book 1, Ch1.28 L205-206)
- Selys notes that Humans do not usually sniff each other, unlike Gnolls. (Book 2, ChS01_Mating_Rituals L313)
- Human women have a monthly bleeding period, which Gnolls and Drakes do not have. (Book 16, ChMating_Rituals_Pt.2 L63-66)
- {belief} Wesle says people in the Human town of Celum do not like Drakes and that most Drakes do not like Humans. (Book 3, Ch3.15 L374)
- Quite a few Drakes live in Human lands even though the Drake cities actively discourage it. (Book 6, Ch4.47 L94)
- Gnolls and Drakes were pushed south by Humans: the Five Families destroyed the last of the northern Walled Cities and drove both peoples south past the High Passes. (Book 6, Ch4.36O L150)
- The Humans took half the continent while Gnolls and Drakes were fighting each other. (Book 6, Ch4.36O L150)
- Humans in northern Izril rarely owned slaves, and ladies such as Magnolia Reinhart ended it in her generation, while slaves are legal in Terandria, Baleros and Rhir. (Book 5, Ch4.06KM L99)
- Rhir has been at war for thousands of years, with a Blighted King as sole ruler fighting the Demon King. (Book 6, Ch1.02C L175)
- Rhir is always at war and always has a Blighted King, Queen or Prince fighting some threat. (Book 4, Ch3.35 L92-94)
- Erin Solstice, an Earther, found herself on this world after turning a corner and suddenly appearing near a dragon. (Book 1, Ch1.07 L105-113)
- Ryoka tells Erin that other Earthers are also in this world, scattered around it. (Book 2, Ch2.02 L78)
- Erin reasons that there are more people from her world than just herself in this one. (Book 2, Ch2.01 L129)
- A group of Earthers, about twenty Americans, was summoned to Rhir and travels with an escort. (Book 6, Ch1.02C L36)
- Every summoned Earther in that group gained the [Hero] class on arrival, at Level 1, which makes them level other classes much faster. (Book 2, Ch1.00C L33)
- The King of Rhir considers the first summoned group a mistake and wants to perform the summoning ritual again to call more heroes. (Book 2, Ch1.00C L308)
- At a banquet in Rhir, Richard and most of the summoned Humans sit with Rhir's nobility while Drakes shout across the hall. (Book 6, Ch4.47 L332)
- The people of Rhir know of the summoned heroes from Earth, though not necessarily in those terms. (Book 18, Ch7.34C L462)
- Several Earthers realise they no longer think about their families, and Siri says she has not thought of hers even once. (Book 10, Ch6.07D L139-148)
- Earthers compare dates and find they come from different times, with Aaron from late 2016 and everyone else from later. (Book 18, Ch7.29B L593)
- An Earther reports that on Earth there are missing-people posters, airport disappearances and a Melbourne security video showing dozens vanishing. (Book 18, Ch7.29B L646-647)
- Seven young Earthers (Kevin, Joseph, Rose, Galina, Troydel, Imani and Leon) who were in Magnolia Reinhart's care reach the inn through its door. (Book 17, Ch7.23LM L189)
- Ryoka took those seven Earthers' electronics and sent them to Erin's inn when assassins attacked Magnolia's estates. (Book 17, Ch7.23LM L241-244)

## Drakes
- Drakes are distantly related to Dragons, and Relc says Dragons are bad news because they eat people. (Book 1, Ch1.07 L112)
- Relc says Drakes can breathe fire and are special because of their relation to Dragons. (Book 1, Ch1.07 L217-219)
- Relc says Drakes prefer drier climates, warm sun and open spaces, unlike lizardfolk who live near water. (Book 1, Ch1.07 L215)
- Drakes insist they are not lizards: they do not lay eggs or hibernate and are descended from Dragons only in some ways. (Book 2, ChS01_Mating_Rituals L295-302)
- Selys says Drakes would not tolerate lizardfolk in their cities. (Book 1, Ch1.28 L248)
- Drakes are mammals with live births and the same reproductive basics as Humans in most respects, though they call their young hatchlings. (Book 13, Ch6.57 L259)
- Drakes have a single male organ, which Drakes call the second tail, and the belief that they have two is a misconception about lizard people. (Book 2, ChS01_Mating_Rituals L95,L105-107)
- Drake women do not menstruate. (Book 16, ChMating_Rituals_Pt.2 L65)
- A Drake's tail is long, muscular and boned, can flex and twist, and can even manipulate objects. (Book 2, ChS01_Mating_Rituals L399-404)
- Drakes use tails to keep balance and express feeling, and Klbkch and Pisces watch Drakes' tails to read their reactions. (Book 3, Ch3.21L L224; Book 6, Ch4.36O L233)
- Drakes have pointed teeth and long tongues, and Selys bites through beef without effort. (Book 2, ChS01_Mating_Rituals L181,L194)
- Drake hatchlings like to bite everything they can fit their mouths around. (Book 2, Ch2.35 L77)
- Drakes in Pallass come in green, red, blue and yellow scales, some vivid and some muted, none with the pale tones of Human skin. (Book 7, Ch5.01 L134)
- A Drake's scales can change colour with embarrassment. (Book 6, Ch4.36O L147)
- Drakes eat a very meaty, fishy and sometimes insect-heavy diet and are not keen on bread, which Gnolls eat. (Book 1, Ch1.29 L155)
- Drakes do not usually bury their dead in stone because it is too expensive, and usually cremate them. (Book 1, Ch1.02H L152)
- Drakes do not live past seventy or eighty years on average, and those who live near or battle monsters die earlier. (Book 13, Ch6.50I L364)
- Magic and potions can extend a Drake's life to a hundred and twenty years, and Ilvriss has heard of Drakes reaching a hundred and forty using potions and artifacts. (Book 13, Ch6.50I L364)
- Most Drakes have no wings and cannot breathe fire, though in the Dragon age when Drakes were young they had wings. (Book 5, Ch4.16 L48-49)
- Some Drake soldiers can still breathe fire, smoke, lightning or mist, or spit acid. (Book 5, Ch4.16 L50,L64,L166)
- Drakes with the ancestral traits are called Oldblood, which is the correct term, and calling them anything else risks a face full of fire or lightning. (Book 5, Ch4.16 L63)
- Oldblood Drakes remember a part of what it was like to be a Dragon because they have more of their ancestor's blood. (Book 5, Ch4.16 L64)
- Less than one in a thousand Drakes shows the old blood, and families with pure bloodlines are far more likely to have an Oldblood child. (Book 5, Ch4.16 L65)
- Oldblood Drakes are cherished, their families are often given money and aid, and having the old blood is as good as a noble title in some circles. (Book 5, Ch4.16 L65-66)
- A company of Oldblood soldiers is called the Ancestor Guard, and one cannot join it without being born to it. (Book 5, Ch4.16 L54-55,L59)
- An Oldblood Drake with both wings and a breath weapon is the rarest combination. (Book 10, Ch6.02 L287)
- The Flamewardens of Pallass have fire breath and the Wings of Pallass can fly. (Book 8, Ch5.43 L523)
- A Gnoll in Pallass repeatedly jumps from the walls with Featherfall Potions and fails, since only some Oldblood Drakes with wings fly from the walls. (Book 7, Ch5.01 L435-438)
- Scorchlings are a kind of Oldblood Drake whose heritage is fire within them, so most die with their mothers and survivors have flaking scales and pain. (Book 6, Ch4.43 L450)
- Scorchlings are shunned. (Book 6, Ch4.43 L451-452)
- Drakes are possessive and greedy, and a saying holds that if only dust were left two Drakes would fight over who had the most. (Book 7, Ch5.16S L347-350)
- Drakes write wills to stop blood feuds over inheritance. (Book 7, Ch5.16S L382)
- Selys says Drake cities fight wars against each other all the time. (Book 7, Ch5.16S L347)
- Drakes have no [Kings], on the principle that no one person should be above others. (Book 6, Ch4.44M L246)
- Walled Cities have ancient three-hundred foot walls, enchanted so the stone hardens and repairs itself, and no army has taken one by siege although coups and betrayals have toppled them. (Book 2, ChS02_The_Antinium_Wars_Pt.1 L79)
- Walled Cities are not all governed the same way, using an elected official, a body of powerful individuals or a hereditary leader. (Book 2, ChS02_The_Antinium_Wars_Pt.1 L37)
- Some Walled Cities are ruled by Lords and Ladies of the Wall, who are Drakes. (Book 9, ChInterlude_Krshia L509)
- The Walled Cities were Drake refuges first built against Dragons, according to Krshia's account of Gnoll memory. (Book 6, Ch4.36O L108)
- Olesm says Drakes kept most of their populations behind Walled Cities and still do, only in smaller and more numerous cities. (Book 6, Ch4.36O L144)
- Among Drakes a Watch Captain generally ranks with a ruling Council member and has complete authority over local military matters. (Book 2, ChS02_The_Antinium_Wars_Pt.1 L24)
- Ilvriss calls Salazsar the premier Walled City and the hub of Drake civilization. (Book 6, Ch4.43 L151)
- Pallass is a Walled City whose people call it the city of inventors and claim it manufactures more arms and potions than any other city on the continent. (Book 7, Ch5.01 L311-314,L326)
- Pallass is built with its urban centre at ground level and four staircases and lifts rising to higher floors within the walls. (Book 7, Ch5.01 L334)
- Pallass has magic lifts powered by mana stones. (Book 7, Ch5.01 L389-390)
- Liscor is a small Drake city on the trade route between north and south, the only way through the mountain range besides the High Passes and coastal routes. (Book 1, Ch1.28 L227; Book 7, Ch5.01 L314)
- Selys says justice in Liscor is harsh and swift and its Watch is stronger than most cities', except maybe the Walled Cities. (Book 2, Ch2.20 L87)
- Drakes in southern cities hold the Watch Captain and Council seats, while Gnolls only sometimes sit in ruling bodies such as Pallass's Senate. (Book 9, ChInterlude_Krshia L509-510)
- Some Drake cities do not tolerate slavery. (Book 5, Ch4.06KM L99)
- Drakes say "Drakes do not run" as a defining saying. (Book 2, Ch2.25 L285; Book 6, Ch4.47 L153)
- Drakes do not take to magic like other species and consider it dishonourable, too easy and not military, according to an old Drake veteran. (Book 6, ChS02_The_Antinium_Wars_Pt.4 L198)
- Krsysl Wordsmith writes that Drakes do not forgive. (Book 6, ChS02_The_Antinium_Wars_Pt.3 L12)
- Krsysl Wordsmith writes that an honest Drake stabs from the front. (Book 6, ChS02_The_Antinium_Wars_Pt.3 L156)
- Drakes and Gnolls warred for hundreds of years, and Olesm believes from histories that the Gnolls were winning. (Book 6, Ch4.36O L138-140)
- Drakes fought the Gnolls even though both peoples were preyed on by Dragons, according to Gnoll memory. (Book 6, Ch4.36O L108)
- {belief} Krshia says Drakes are stubborn and rigid, but their records let them see the past while Gnolls only remember fragments. (Book 9, ChInterlude_Krshia L444)
- {belief} A speaker says Drake cities are independent bodies that do not hold hands like Humans. (Book 2, Ch2.41 L367)
- The Drake cities, humbled by the Antinium, rebuilt and strengthened garrisons before 13 A.F. (Book 6, ChS02_The_Antinium_Wars_Pt.3 L13)

## Gnolls
- Gnolls are tall, furred, hyena-like people; Krshia stands six feet tall with a mouth full of sharp canine teeth. (Book 2, ChS01_Mating_Rituals L47,L193)
- Gnolls have no physical difference in body mass between the sexes, as Pisces notes while comparing them with Minotaurs and Garuda. (Book 13, Ch6.57 L256)
- Gnolls greet by showing their teeth. (Book 1, Ch1.32 L52)
- Gnolls usually say "yes" at the end of sentences and say "no" instead only when upset or angry. (Book 2, Ch2.28 L250)
- Gnolls may howl only at a full moon, and they are forbidden to howl otherwise. (Book 1, Ch1.21 L115)
- A Gnoll's hackles rise when angry. (Book 1, Ch1.35 L134)
- Gnolls can hear four times better than a Human or Drake. (Book 9, ChInterlude_Krshia L3)
- Gnolls smell when a female is in estrus or pregnant, and they have little modesty about mating because everyone can smell it. (Book 2, ChS01_Mating_Rituals L280-282)
- Gnolls can smell fear, which makes leading them hard when you are afraid. (Book 8, Ch5.42 L587)
- Plains Gnolls have stronger noses than city Gnolls, who lack practice smelling rain or animals underground. (Book 8, Ch5.42 L630)
- Gnolls lack periods but have estrus periods. (Book 16, ChMating_Rituals_Pt.2 L63-66)
- Gnoll mating involves a knot, so sex can last half an hour or more. (Book 2, ChS01_Mating_Rituals L254-258)
- Gnolls brew a tea that prevents pregnancy. (Book 2, ChS01_Mating_Rituals L288-291)
- Gnoll cubs are pretty animal-like. (Book 2, Ch2.35 L77)
- Gnoll fur colours have meaning, and white is not a natural colour for Gnolls of any tribe. (Book 4, Ch3.27M L78)
- White fur is a mark of dire fate for Gnolls, claimed by some to be a curse and a sign of disaster. (Book 4, Ch3.27M L313)
- Some Gnolls are born with white fur and others lose their colour after terrible events, and Gnolls fear such individuals. (Book 4, Ch3.27M L316)
- A Gnoll calls Erin's white-furred Mrsha a cursed doombringer. (Book 4, Ch3.38 L146)
- Gnolls live in tribes that wander the plains of Izril and the continent, and there are no Gnoll cities. (Book 9, ChInterlude_Krshia L2; Book 15, Ch7.10K L570)
- Each tribe has a [Shaman] chosen to wield the pack's magic. (Book 1, Ch1.34 L289)
- Chieftain Nelrra is both a [Chieftain] and a [Shaman] and leans on a walking staff. (Book 15, Ch7.10K L575)
- Gnolls choose who casts magic, as a tribe chooses one shaman, in contrast to Human magic. (Book 1, Ch1.34 L289)
- A [Shaman] has no magic to cast without other Gnolls to draw on. (Book 1, Ch1.35 L135)
- There has been no Gnoll [Mage] in living memory and Gnoll [Mages] are thought impossible. (Book 7, Ch5.06M L153-155)
- Gnolls refuse to trade with Wistram over an old grudge. (Book 7, Ch5.06M L154)
- Krshia first tells Erin that the Wistram insult happened nearly twenty years ago, at a gathering of the tribes. (Book 1, Ch1.35 L131)
- Later, Krshia tells Mrsha the Wistram incident was forty years ago and that her representative lasted less than a year. (Book 9, ChInterlude_Krshia L463)
- No Gnoll in the tribes has become a [Mage] for at least a few centuries, before the Wistram feud, and the cause is unknown. (Book 9, ChInterlude_Krshia L465)
- Gnoll tribes meet once every ten years in a Meeting of Tribes. (Book 1, Ch1.35 L131; Book 2, Ch2.34 L228)
- At the decennial gathering each tribe brings a gift or item of value to raise its voice. (Book 2, Ch2.20 L207-208; Book 2, Ch2.34 L228)
- Worth governs Gnolls, more than seniority or power. (Book 2, Ch2.20 L203)
- Gnolls have a half-language that works as a secret tongue for warning each other, and only Goblins and the people of Drath have wholly separate languages. (Book 14, Ch6.63P L30)
- Gnoll history is spoken and passed from Gnoll to Gnoll, and told differently from Drake history. (Book 6, Ch4.36O L105)
- {belief} Gnoll memory says Izril was once the home of Dragons, who with Drakes preyed on Gnolls. (Book 6, Ch4.36O L105-108)
- {belief} Gnoll memory says Gnolls hid in caverns and lived underground, and some of them changed there. (Book 6, Ch4.36O L110-115)
- Gnolls fought Drakes for hundreds of years, were strongest in the north, and destroyed many of the northern Walled Cities. (Book 6, Ch4.36O L138-140)
- The Gnoll population is a fraction of what it was, but tribes grow and replenish, and Krshia hints that they may reclaim old lands in a few generations. (Book 6, Ch4.36O L153,L158)
- When Gnolls make war they make it as a people and do not lay down arms until all tribes agree. (Book 6, Ch4.36O L135)
- Gnolls don't like working with other races as much as Drakes and Humans, so fewer become adventurers. (Book 1, Ch1.28 L204)
- Chieftain Nelrra leads a small tribe joined with other small tribes, and they are weary of Izril and of conflict with the Drakes. (Book 15, Ch7.10K L562-565)
- Nelrra says not all Drake cities fight the tribes, but tribes fight Drake cities, are accused of thievery and feud among themselves over old grudges. (Book 15, Ch7.10K L570-573)
- City Gnolls live in Drake cities, and the plains tribes sometimes use the term with pity or disdain. (Book 9, ChInterlude_Krshia L2)
- Rufelt says a Plains Gnoll might call a City Gnoll a domesticated fool without tribe or loyalty. (Book 10, Ch6.09 L461)
- City Gnolls are free of tribe loyalty and are not born into a family of hundreds or thousands who all have connections to them. (Book 10, Ch6.09 L461)
- City Gnolls are pickier about their garb than Plains Gnolls, and a few go about fully dressed. (Book 10, Ch6.08 L39)
- City Gnolls are not true [Shamans], though they have tricks. (Book 9, ChInterlude_Krshia L640)
- City Gnolls form secret ruling bodies in Drake cities that decide how Gnolls behave. (Book 9, ChInterlude_Krshia L510)
- A City Gnoll council has no [Chieftain] but a body of the oldest, wisest or highest-level Gnolls, and its members are often ordinary [Butchers], [Bakers] and [Chandlers]. (Book 9, ChInterlude_Krshia L512,L515)
- In Liscor one Gnoll representative speaks for several thousand Gnolls, and in Pallass one speaks for ten thousand. (Book 9, ChInterlude_Krshia L514)
- Krshia led a large portion of the Silverfang tribe to Liscor ten years ago and leads the City Gnolls council. (Book 9, ChInterlude_Krshia L516)
- Gnolls sent a delegation to Liscor nearly a decade ago to gain information or objects of value for tribal standing. (Book 2, Ch2.34 L228)
- Gnolls eat bread, unlike Drakes. (Book 1, Ch1.29 L155)

## Antinium
- Antinium are a species that came from Rhir, where the Blighted King's armies fought them, and in 5 B.F. they vanished from that continent. (Book 2, ChS02_The_Antinium_Wars_Pt.1 L14)
- Of two hundred greatships that left Rhir carrying Antinium and Queens, only four ships reached the southwestern edge of Izril, in the last month of winter. (Book 2, ChS02_The_Antinium_Wars_Pt.1 L17,L20-21)
- The Antinium are poor sailors and nearly all their ships were lost at sea. (Book 2, ChS02_The_Antinium_Wars_Pt.1 L22)
- A history in the Wandering Inn's library says the First Incursion War was a decade-long war that razed cities and forced Gnoll tribes to retreat. (Book 1, Ch1.04R L10)
- Antinium cannot swim or float, and they die of suffocation after holding their breath, which became the weapon against them. (Book 2, ChS02_The_Antinium_Wars_Pt.2 L94)
- By the end of the first war the Antinium had built four Hives, and a destroyed Hive left a dry desert with no vegetation or wildlife in a fifty mile radius. (Book 2, ChS02_The_Antinium_Wars_Pt.2 L111-112)
- Eight years after the first war the Antinium hold six Hives and a foothold in southern Izril, which are forbidden zones, and border-crossers are executed. (Book 6, ChS02_The_Antinium_Wars_Pt.3 L10-11,L16)
- The year of the Second Antinium War is 13 A.F. (Book 6, ChS02_The_Antinium_Wars_Pt.3 L13)
- At one point only seven Queens were left out of what had been hundreds. (Book 6, ChS02_The_Antinium_Wars_Pt.3 L69)
- Over three hundred Queens left Rhir, along with their warrior-queens and the Centenium, to flee the sleeping god, and most drowned at sea. (Book 5, Ch4.24 L189-191)
- Of the eight Queens that survived the crossing, six remain. (Book 5, Ch4.24 L192)
- Antinium think as a hive mind, and a hundred Workers can play chess as one mind. (Book 1, Ch1.27 L151-156)
- Pawn says the Workers felt Klbkch's and another Worker's death and remember them, since as long as one Antinium exists none truly dies. (Book 1, Ch1.27 L158-164)
- Antinium do not level much: Workers rarely level and some never do. (Book 1, Ch1.27 L60-62)
- Only Queens level and almost only in the [Queen] class. (Book 2, Ch2.36 L83)
- Antinium lack magical affinity as a deliberate part of their creation, and since they do not level greatly magic is not seen as a viable field. (Book 4, Ch3.32 L197-199)
- Antinium are glucose-intolerant, so pasta and bread make them lethargic and ill. (Book 1, Ch1.21 L19-20)
- Antinium can survive freezing weather for up to two hours before needing warmth. (Book 2, Ch2.11 L192)
- Most Antinium wear no clothing, and only those who go above ground wear loincloths because other species make a fuss. (Book 16, ChMating_Rituals_Pt.2 L543)
- Antinium recycle their waste within the Hive. (Book 1, Ch1.33 L154)
- Pawn's severed arms and leg are regrowing after a battle. (Book 2, Ch2.11 L194-195)
- Antinium have no sexual organs: they are smooth chitin with only a disposal valve. (Book 16, ChMating_Rituals_Pt.2 L545-548,L1159)
- Queens produce eggs as a matter of course, which is why they grow so large to sustain a Hive's population. (Book 5, Ch4.24 L187)
- Birthers are Antinium designed for one purpose, and in later years pod-like Birthers take the place of Queens in creating Antinium. (Book 5, Ch4.24 L187; Book 18, Ch7.39A L805)
- A Birther sac has no real consciousness, is designed only to create, and is fragile. (Book 18, Ch7.39A L1321-1323)
- The Armored Queen has birthed more Antinium than any other living Queen and has grown huge and unable to move. (Book 18, Ch7.39A L802-806)
- Antinium believe nothing follows death, as an Antinium's memory remains in the Hive while the Antinium themselves cease to be. (Book 2, Ch2.26 L472-474)
- A newly grown Soldier is said to be the equivalent of a Level 15 [Warrior], and its carapace is equivalent to unenchanted plate armor. (Book 1, Ch1.38 L154-155)
- {belief} Selys says the giant Soldier types do not need levels because they are deadly enough as they are. (Book 1, Ch1.27 L72-74)
- Pawn, a Worker, refers to himself as "this one". (Book 1, Ch1.27 L151)
- Soldiers cannot speak. (Book 3, Ch3.06L L38)
- Soldiers eat sitting at tables made of packed dirt because their food basins are deep and their clumsier appendages struggle to balance containers. (Book 3, Ch3.06L L179-182)
- Antinium do not have names, and Selys says one should never ask a Worker's name. (Book 1, Ch1.26 L186-190)
- A crowd of nearly a hundred Workers offered Erin ritual condolences in a procession. (Book 1, Ch1.26 L147-150)
- Workers wonder whether the new game of chess is an Aberration, but decide it is not because the Propugnator did not eliminate the twelve who learned it. (Book 1, Ch1.25 L5)
- An Aberration is an Antinium that is individual, rejects the Hive and kills. (Book 1, Ch1.36 L305)
- A Prognugator is a leader meant to direct Soldiers and handle unexpected situations. (Book 1, Ch1.38 L177)
- A Prognugator judges other Antinium, and their lives are his to use. (Book 1, Ch1.39 L154)
- Klbkch becomes a Revalantor, technically above the rank of Prognugator, and can ignore even his Queen's instructions. (Book 2, Ch2.15 L5)
- The Centenium are ageless champions of old whom the Antinium sent from Rhir to the new world. (Book 5, Ch4.24 L190)
- Xrn, of the Centenium, is a blue-carapaced female and a Prognugator to the Grand Queen. (Book 4, Ch3.34 L373,L385)
- Klbkch says the Workers and Soldiers wear the form of Galuc the Builder, one of the Centenium. (Book 3, Ch3.06L L575)
- Klbkch says that his original task as a Centenium relates to Gods and supersedes all other orders. (Book 3, Ch3.06L L266)
- Custodium are partial Prognugators with natural blades, according to Xrn. (Book 3, Ch3.23L L433-434)
- Each Hive's Queen has special dispensation to create her own unique type of Antinium, and the Grand Queen knows every Hive's secrets. (Book 2, Ch2.34 L297)
- The Silent Antinium are an assassin breed with whiter, translucent carapaces, scythe-like arms and mandibles for tearing out a throat. (Book 3, Ch3.23L L427-428)
- Armored Antinium Soldiers have heavily reinforced carapaces and stand two feet taller than regular Soldiers. (Book 3, Ch3.23L L430)
- The Flying Antinium of Pivr have wings, though the Free Queen calls them half-baked copies of the true winged Soldiers of long ago. (Book 4, Ch3.34 L377)
- The Silent Queen can make hexapodal Antinium like the mantis-like Xeu, which is beyond the Flying Queen's design. (Book 18, Ch7.39A L57)
- The Antinium have a Grand Queen, a Silent Queen, an Armored Queen, a Flying Queen, a Twisted Queen and the Free Queen. (Book 18, Ch7.39A L321,L368,L802,L1073,L1323)
- Klbkch is Prognugator of the Free Antinium of Liscor, whose Queen and Klbkch work to bring individuality to the Antinium. (Book 1, Ch1.38 L150; Book 8, Ch5.33B L89)
- The Free Queen was exiled to Liscor with few resources because her goals went against her peers. (Book 8, Ch5.33B L89)
- Individuals such as Pawn, Garry, Anand and Belgrade are not true Antinium in the Free Queen's view and are tools for her and Klbkch. (Book 8, Ch5.33B L88)
- Klbkch regards Individuals as more than half-made Soldiers and Workers, but not True Antinium. (Book 8, Ch5.39 L533)
- Klbkch says that if he could turn a thousand Antinium into Individuals of Belgrade's or Anand's character, he would do it instantly. (Book 8, Ch5.33B L308)
- Painted Soldiers are painted so each is unique, and Klbkch says the purpose is that they are Individual. (Book 4, Ch3.34 L439,L448)
- Liscor cheers the Painted Soldiers in the parade for the first time in history, with Drakes, Gnolls and Humans all applauding the Antinium. (Book 7, Ch5.13 L408-417)
- Soldiers' names and identities came when they painted themselves. (Book 19, Ch7.50 L172)

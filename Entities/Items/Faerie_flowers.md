# Faerie flowers
> Type: item | Also called: Faerie Flowers; Alice Flowers; golden flowers | First appears: Book 2, Ch2.20
> See also: Frost Faeries, Erin Solstice, Xif
## Overview
- Faerie flowers are small yellow flowers, much like short dandelions with more exotic petals, that Erin Solstice received from faeries in place of gold. (Book 2, Ch2.20 L2-5; Book 12, Ch6.38 L515)
- Erin realizes that the faeries tricked her with flowers instead of gold, as faeries do in the stories. (Book 2, Ch2.20 L3-5)
- The flowers are golden and soft to touch, and Ceria expected gold before realizing it was a trick. (Book 2, Ch2.20 L296)
## Effects
- Erin tastes the nectar gathered where the petals meet, which is tasteless but fizzes on her tongue, and she shrinks until the room towers above her. (Book 2, Ch2.20 L30-35)
- Erin calls the flowers Alice Flowers after the drink in Alice in Wonderland, and she is shrunk for the better part of an hour. (Book 2, Ch2.20 L36,L296-297)
- Ceria senses the same tingling in her mind from the flowers that she gets from the Frost Faeries. (Book 2, Ch2.20 L298)
- Selys confirms that Erin thought the faeries had paid her in golden coins, and the narrator explains that faeries trick people into accepting gold that turns to flowers or disappears the next day. (Book 2, Ch2.33 L547-554)
- Lyonette knows the flowers can be made into a drink, and the adventurer Halrac paid gold for it. (Book 3, Ch3.21L L300)
- Erin has worried about the Faerie Flower drink's power after seeing its effect on Halrac and Ilvriss. (Book 12, Ch6.38 L516)
## At the inn
- {state} While Erin is away, her potted golden flowers wilt, and Lyonette throws a dead flower into the fireplace and wakes on her back after coughing on its acrid smoke. (Book 3, Ch3.21L L298-305)
- Later, yellow Faerie Flowers grow in boxes along the inn's windowsills, which Mrsha waters every morning with one cup, and Lyonette uses a few each week to gather honey. (Book 7, Ch5.06M L56-59)
- Apista the Ashfire Bee seems addicted to the tiny flowers and laps at them with her tongue. (Book 7, Ch5.06M L60)
- Over the months since winter the flowers have multiplied and now fill eight planter boxes. (Book 12, Ch6.38 L515,L547-550)
- Xif the [Alchemist] examines the flowers, says they all lack the characteristics of full-grown plants such as reproductive elements, and that he believes they are not fully grown. (Book 12, Ch6.38 L515,L552-558)
- Erin realizes that the flowers are just stalks with blooms on the end, with no root system and no ovules. (Book 12, Ch6.38 L559)

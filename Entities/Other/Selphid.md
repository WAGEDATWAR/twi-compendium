# Selphid
> Type: creature | Also called: Selphids | First appears: Book 2, Ch2.22
> See also: Jelaqua, Okasha, Geneva Scale
## Overview
- Jelaqua says Selphids are a species that find dead bodies and inhabit them, a kind of parasite or body thief, which is the only way they can live. (Book 2, Ch2.22 L422,L426)
- Ceria explains to Erin that Selphids could rudely be called parasites. (Book 2, Ch2.22 L421)
- Jelaqua is a Selphid inhabiting a nearly albino dead body, and she shows Erin the writhing dark green and yellow creatures with thin tendrils inside her chest. (Book 2, Ch2.22 L433-436)
- Jelaqua says Selphids do not inhabit living people as a rule, take on the attributes of their host, and can change bodies if one gets damaged. (Book 2, Ch2.22 L438)
- Jelaqua says healing potions do not work on dead flesh. (Book 2, Ch2.22 L438)
## Anatomy
- Geneva Scale finds a Selphid stretched through a dead man's nervous system, a green wriggling mass of tendrils wrapped around his organs. (Book 3, Ch1.01D L152-153)
- The Selphid is semi-fluid, so stitches would be as useless as trying to block water with a net, and it bleeds a viscous orange liquid. (Book 3, Ch1.01D L155-157)
- Geneva cauterizes the Selphid with hot metal, which makes it shriek, and the bleeding stops. (Book 3, Ch1.01D L164)
## In armies
- {belief} A soldier tells Geneva that Selphids are body-snatchers and parasites that live in corpses and move them about, and that "Selphids aren't people". (Book 3, Ch1.01D L127,L133)
- After Geneva puts the Selphid into a dead woman's body, the woman's skin turns totally white and the right half of her body sags as if from a stroke. (Book 3, Ch1.01D L229-230)
- The Selphid names herself Okasha, a Level 23 [Rogue] of the Burning March 6th Division, and says it takes Selphids time to assume control of a new form. (Book 3, Ch1.01D L234,L239)
- {event} Jelaqua slithers out of Moore's open wound into a Raskghar's body, begging the others to say they saw nothing, and Bevussa promises that no one will tell. (Book 9, Ch5.58 L742-752)
- Pisces understands that invading a living host, willing or not, is the height of Selphid sin. (Book 9, Ch5.58 L753)

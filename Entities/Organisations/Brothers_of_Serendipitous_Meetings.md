# Brothers of Serendipitous Meetings
> Type: org | Also called: the Brothers; Gentlemen Callers | First appears: Book 9, Ch5.54
> See also: Undersea Crews
## Overview
- The Brothers of Serendipitous Meetings are an underworld organisation of Izril with a reputation for being polite. (Book 9, Ch5.54 L915)
- The Brothers and the Sisters of Chell were not the biggest underworld groups in Izril, but the two largest local players in the region by far. (Book 9, Ch5.54 L916)
- {event} A thug of the Brothers presented the group's regards, saying he was choosing his moment to help. (Book 9, Ch5.54 L891)
- {event} The Brothers' and Sisters' boats, driven by Skills, fled across the water past Liscor's walls, but a bolt of lightning exploded the Sisters' boat and threw the Brothers forward. (Book 9, Ch5.54 L947,L956)
## Later dealings
- {event} Niers Astoragon ordered a message sent to the Brothers of Serendipitous Meetings, offering a proposition and a gift of fine wine. (Book 10, Ch6.07D L746)
- The Brothers wanted to know what happened to their group as badly as the Sisters of Chell did, and had a second motive in a very lucrative offer from the Tallman of Baleros. (Book 12, ChInterlude_Numbtongue_Pt.1 L242)
- A man in an alleyway said he represented the Brothers of Serendipitous Meetings and proposed an arrangement. (Book 17, ChInterlude_The_Gecko_of_Illusions L910)
- Wilovan is a member of the Brothers, and his untoward actions would not befit him. (Book 18, ChInterlude_Meetings_and_Friendships L101)
- Palt explained to Erin that the Gentlemen Callers are two big Faces who are part of the Brothers, a smaller but elite gang that are big players in northern Izril. (Book 19, Ch7.52 L265)
- Wilovan said that as members of the Brothers they follow a code: if they prey on those with coin, it is never those down on their luck, never children, never decent sorts. (Book 19, Ch7.52 L274)
- The Gentlemen Callers, as representatives of the Brothers, inspected two rooms at Erin's inn, and Erin thought they might not be good guys but were trustworthy to a certain degree. (Book 19, Ch7.53 L1193)

# Yesel
> Type: person | Also called: Miss Yesel; Prost's wife | First appears: Book 3, Ch3.00E
> See also: Prost, Laken, Durene, Riverfarm
## Overview
- Yesel is the wife of Prost, the [Farmer] of Riverfarm. (Book 3, Ch3.00E L403)
- Yesel gives Durene a basket full of goods to take home after Laken first visits the village. (Book 3, Ch3.00E L403)
## Visit to Durene's cottage (Book 3)
- Yesel visits Laken and Durene at the cottage, bringing a gift of clothes, and chats with Laken over mint tea. (Book 3, Ch3.01E L109,L131,L147)
- Yesel offers to put Laken up in her own home, with the children squeezing into one room. (Book 3, Ch3.01E L110)
- Yesel offers to have Durene pull a wagon to carry Laken to the village. (Book 3, Ch3.01E L117)
- Yesel says that the Beetrs would also welcome Laken, since they have an empty room after their daughter died the previous summer. (Book 3, Ch3.01E L121)
- Laken declines every offer, and he senses Yesel's invisible looks at Durene and her reactions across the table. (Book 3, Ch3.01E L112-127)
- Yesel sends Durene out for firewood so she can speak to Laken alone. (Book 3, Ch3.01E L129-131)
- Yesel tells Laken that the villagers put Durene out at the cottage so she would not cause trouble, and asks whether Durene has told him what she is. (Book 3, Ch3.01E L132)
- Yesel presses Laken to stay in the village, and he stops her by saying he prefers to let people keep their secrets. (Book 3, Ch3.01E L139-145)
- Laken sends Yesel out of the cottage, thanking her for the clothes. (Book 3, Ch3.01E L147-148)
## Later (Book 5-12)
- Laken asks after Yesel and the children, and Prost answers that all are well. (Book 3, Ch3.11E L229-230)
- Durene eats fresh bread that Prost's wife Yesel baked in an oven the villagers uncovered that morning. (Book 5, Ch4.21E L274)
- Yesel tells Ryoka that the villagers still remember her Christmas presents for the little ones during the hard winter. (Book 12, Ch6.41E L148-149)

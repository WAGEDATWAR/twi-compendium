# Sir Nil Moxae
> Type: person | Also called: Sir Nil | First appears: Book 8, Ch5.22G
> See also: Bethal, Kerrig, Sir Thomast, Dame Truvia Cartiil, Redfang Goblins
## Overview
- Sir Nil Moxae is one of Lady Bethal Walchaís's Rose Knights, a [Knight] wearing pink armour who carries a sword and shield. (Book 8, Ch5.22G L97,L126; Book 8, Ch5.23G L4)
- Sir Nil Moxae's companion among the Rose Knights is Dame Truvia Cartiil, who carries a spear. (Book 8, Ch5.22G L97)
## The Goblin ambush
- Sir Nil raises the alarm at movement in the trees as Lady Bethal's party rides through the forest. (Book 8, Ch5.22G L96-97)
- Sir Nil and Dame Truvia ride ahead to scout and are stopped after about a hundred yards, when Sir Nil reports two dozen mounted Goblins ahead. (Book 8, Ch5.22G L121-124)
- Sir Nil proposes to draw the Goblins off by attacking from the side, and Lady Bethal refuses, saying they break through together or not at all. (Book 8, Ch5.22G L130-132)
- Sir Nil turns to face the first Goblin Carn Wolf rider and utters a challenge as mounted Goblins surround the four Humans. (Book 8, Ch5.22G L147)
- Sir Nil and Dame Truvia shield Lady Bethal with their bodies while over a hundred Goblins aim crossbows at her. (Book 8, Ch5.23G L3-4,L6)
- Sir Nil blocks a stray crossbow bolt aimed at Lady Bethal's chest with his shield, shattering it. (Book 8, Ch5.23G L17-18)
- Sir Nil rides forward when the Goblins demand that the Humans drop their weapons, and the Goblins back away. (Book 8, Ch5.23G L36-38)
- Sir Nil sheathes his sword with Thomast and Truvia when Lady Bethal nods her assent to the Hob's demand. (Book 8, Ch5.23G L39-40)
- Dame Truvia suggests that Sir Nil stay on the road with Lady Bethal, but Bethal insists they all go together to find Sir Kerrig. (Book 8, Ch5.23G L26,L46-47)
## Aftermath
- Sir Nil dismounts to check the churned ground at the ambush site and coughs from the lingering poison. (Book 8, Ch5.23G L62-63)
- Sir Nil clasps Sir Kerrig's arm and urges him to mount his stallion. (Book 8, Ch5.23G L80)
- Sir Nil bows his head at once when Lady Bethal sharply tells him to let Sir Kerrig speak. (Book 8, Ch5.23G L83-84)

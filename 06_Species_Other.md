# Other Sentient Species
> Scope: every sentient species or people except Humans/Earthers, Drakes, Gnolls, Antinium, Goblins and Raskghar (see 05_Species_Core.md). Non-sentient animals and monsters are in 07_Creatures_and_Monsters.md; named individuals belong to character files.
> Keys: Frost Faeries, Fae, Vampires, Selphids, Dullahans, Half-Elves, Lizardfolk, Garuda, String People, Centaurs, Minotaurs and others.
> See also: 05_Species_Core.md, 07_Creatures_and_Monsters.md

## Frost Faeries and the Fae
- Frost Faeries bring the winter, flying over the land and freezing it as they go. (Book 2, Ch2.09 L9,L12)
- Ryoka thinks that in this world winter is a product of creatures rather than weather, and that the faeries seem slow and inefficient at it. (Book 2, Ch2.14 L38)
- {belief} People in Liscor regard the Frost Faeries as a force of nature rather than actual creatures. (Book 2, Ch2.10 L109)
- Frost Faeries are also called Winter Sprites. (Book 3, Ch3.03 L21; Book 5, Ch4.16 L98)
- Frost Faeries look humanoid, with bodies that are part crystal and part ice and insect-like wings, and are breathtakingly beautiful. (Book 3, Ch3.03 L21-22)
- The fairies' anatomy differs from Humans' and half-Elves': they are made of what looks like fluid ice and pure crystal. (Book 2, Ch2.08 L288)
- Frost Faeries glow blue in the dark. (Book 2, Ch2.16 L440)
- A Frost Faerie has wickedly sharp teeth in a tiny mouth. (Book 2, Ch2.18 L415)
- Their magic makes them fuzzy blobs to nearly everyone in this world, and they cannot be heard or barely be seen, so they are generally treated like natural phenomena. (Book 3, Ch3.03 L28,L92)
- Ryoka Griffin can see and hear the Frost Faeries, which Garia cannot, though Garia still sees something. (Book 2, Ch2.09 L204,L228)
- Later, people of this world are described as looking a bit to the left of a faerie, at its glamour. (Book 5, Ch4.17 L35)
- Frost Faeries delight in pranks: they throw snow, freeze hair stiff and drop frozen beetles on heads. (Book 3, Ch3.03 L29; Book 2, Ch2.18 L341)
- The faeries are called vicious, mischievous and deceitful by Ryoka. (Book 3, Ch3.03 L105)
- Frost Faeries cannot stand being indoors in a building made with iron, and hiss and leap off Ryoka at the Runner's Guild door. (Book 3, Ch3.03 L110)
- Iron in a home is a problem for a faerie even when invited in. (Book 5, Ch4.17 L37)
- Teriarch's cave booms "Pests. Begone!" and a stream of fire blasts overhead, making the Frost Faeries scream and flee. (Book 2, Ch2.12 L243,L251-253)
- Faeries can be bribed with food: Erin's crepes are set out for them, and Ryoka calls them easy to bribe. (Book 3, Ch3.03 L193,L197; Book 5, Ch4.09 L85)
- Ryoka believes, from legend, that she should never thank a faerie. (Book 3, Ch3.03 L469)
- Erin invites the Frost Faeries in with a banquet, following the forms of folk stories. (Book 2, Ch2.19 L377,L380)
- A Frost Faerie named Ivolethe travels with Ryoka and is called her friend. (Book 5, Ch4.09 L72; Book 5, Ch4.17 L24; Book 5, Ch4.29 L333)
- Ivolethe is a Frost Faerie only to the people of this world; her true nature is not Human and not mortal. (Book 5, Ch4.11 L552)
- Frost Faeries cannot tell the future, but they predict it somewhat better than Humans can. (Book 5, Ch4.24 L4-5)
- Ivolethe is immortal, and in one moment shows an immortal's distant look at the world. (Book 5, Ch4.29 L332-333)
- The faeries are not allowed to interfere in events and risk punishment for breaking the rules. (Book 5, Ch4.28 L112; Book 5, Ch4.30 L450)
- Ivolethe teaches Ryoka faerie magic, which starts with sensing the wind. (Book 5, Ch4.17 L247,L251)
- Ivolethe can be carried by the wind as well as fly. (Book 5, Ch4.17 L390)
- Faerie flowers, small yellow flowers, grow at Erin's inn and their nectar goes into drinks such as the inn's Faerie Flower ale. (Book 6, Ch4.45 L150,L156; Book 7, Ch5.06M L56)
- Erin first received the flowers as fake gold coins, a faerie's trick payment. (Book 10, Ch6.08 L128)
- Faerie flower nectar is given to Mrsha in her milk to help with nightmares. (Book 9, Ch5.49 L413)
- At Laken's banquet the fae are described as fair folk who obey bargains and do not lie but twist the truth, and who are harmless if not deceived or threatened. (Book 7, Ch5.11E L246)
- A fae woman bleeds on a steel blade without pain and demands that duelists bring cold iron and star metal. (Book 7, Ch5.11E L512-513)
- Teriarch says the faeries are not from this plane of existence and travel between worlds, and that, as he is told, in some places the will of the world and fate can punish those who interfere, hence the fae keep and enforce their own rules. (Book 2, Ch2.39 L426-428)
- A fae says she suffers a punishment from "my King" for breaking the rules. (Book 7, Ch5.11E L424)
- The fae say the Faerie King does not interfere in this world's doings but remembers, and bends his rules to allow them these gatherings. (Book 7, Ch5.11E L649)

## Vampires
- {belief} Legend says vampires are creatures of death, blood feeders and masters of the night. (Book 10, Ch6.01 L160)
- Vampires are alive rather than undead, with a very slow pulse and cold skin, and see well in the dark without getting cold. (Book 10, Ch6.01 L171,L325,L330-331)
- Vampires are immortal in the legends, but the Drakle family say they live into their sixties or eighties at most. (Book 10, Ch6.01 L379)
- Ryoka calls vampires immortal, quick and strong, after Fierre choked her and did not flinch when Ryoka hit her. (Book 10, Ch6.01 L192)
- Vampires heal fast, need less sleep, and are hard to poison. (Book 10, Ch6.01 L277,L318)
- Food does little for vampires; blood is what they need to live. (Book 10, Ch6.01 L441)
- The Drakle line drink animal blood, bottled, and the family has sworn never to drink from people. (Book 10, Ch6.01 L240; Book 18, ChInterlude_Carriages_and_Conversations L89)
- Biting does not make new vampires. (Book 10, Ch6.01 L240)
- The Drakle line is born vampire and gives birth, and rarely marries outside the family. (Book 10, Ch6.01 L444)
- Vampires do not sleep in coffins. (Book 10, Ch6.01 L149,L160)
- Vampires of the old days included necromancers and stronger vampires who burned up in sunlight like kindling. (Book 10, Ch6.01 L444)
- Vampires have the old stories of Vampire Lords who could turn into mist and had Skills, but Fierre says she has never levelled. (Book 18, Ch7.41 L82)
- Vampires cannot level, which some think was given for balance since a levelling vampire would be unmatched. (Book 12, Ch6.42E L225)
- Vampires used to prey on people and control much of Izril until they were nearly wiped out. (Book 10, Ch6.01 L379)
- Ylawes Byres says vampires used to be a scourge on Izril, with large infestations wiped out over the centuries by House Byres, whose silver was key. (Book 6, Ch4.45 L400)
- {belief} Ylawes says vampires are gone, or no longer number thousands as they once did. (Book 6, Ch4.45 L403)
- Modern vampires are sick and losing their powers, and blood of any species does not stop the decline. (Book 12, Ch6.42E L335-337)
- All vampires long for real people's blood, and most have tried it in secret, but they live on animal blood. (Book 12, Ch6.42E L325-330)
- Fierre, as she walks into the shadows, is described as already getting a tan. (Book 11, Ch6.25 L168)
- Lady Suvelta Reinhart was known to the Circle of Thorns as a Vampire with levels who ruled Izril too long; her death was held to be the end of the Vampires. (Book 17, Ch7.24 L35-36)
- Ylawes Byres, of the family that hunted vampires, dines with Ryoka and Fierre in Book 18, and Fierre hides from him. (Book 18, Ch7.37 L61-62)

## Selphids
- Selphids are creatures that live inside dead bodies, and call themselves parasites and thieves of bodies. (Book 2, Ch2.22 L422,L426)
- Selphids are also called the body snatchers of Baleros, which is a pejorative phrase. (Book 10, Ch6.06D L33)
- A Selphid inside a body shows as writhing dark green and yellow tendrils, which Erin first sees in Jelaqua's chest. (Book 2, Ch2.22 L435-436)
- A Selphid takes on the attributes of its host, and can change bodies when one is damaged because healing potions do not work on dead flesh. (Book 2, Ch2.22 L438)
- Selphid blood is a viscous orange liquid, and a Selphid can be cauterised after being injured. (Book 3, Ch1.01D L155,L160-165)
- A Selphid can die if it cannot get into another body after its host is destroyed. (Book 3, Ch1.01D L131)
- {belief} Some soldiers say Selphids are not people. (Book 3, Ch1.01D L133)
- Selphid hosts look deathly pale (Jelaqua's insides are bloodless and nearly white), and a Selphid that is not speaking breathes very rarely and blinks much less than normal people. (Book 3, Ch1.01D L253; Book 4, ChWistram_Days_Pt.5 L15; Book 2, Ch2.22 L435)
- Selphids pay the families of the dead for the bodies they use and hop from body to body as the old ones decay, like hermit crabs. (Book 10, Ch6.06D L33,L36)
- Selphids never inhabit living creatures; doing so is punishable by death by every species in the world, especially Selphids themselves. (Book 10, Ch6.06D L36)
- Selphids once inhabited living bodies and created a terrible empire, which was overthrown, and any Selphid found doing it is since put to death along with the host. (Book 5, Ch1.06D L124-126)
- Lizardfolk say Selphids never fight each other even when hired on opposite sides, and that few remain because many were killed long ago. (Book 5, Ch1.03D L164-166)
- Selphids are unpopular on Izril, and even less so on Baleros, because of rumours about their kind. (Book 4, Ch3.27M L228)
- {belief} Pisces cites a rumour that you cannot let a Selphid into a graveyard. (Book 4, ChWistram_Days_Pt.2 L113-114)
- Selphids are among the newly accepted Wistram students, including Drake, Human and Lizardman Selphid hosts. (Book 4, ChWistram_Days_Pt.5 L10,L15-16)
- Selphids work as [Guards] and an [Honor Guard] for hire in Baleros. (Book 10, Ch6.06D L27,L63)
- Selphids can pilot a body's muscles to exercise them, which Geneva thinks surpasses modern medical technology. (Book 10, Ch6.06D L436,L440)
- Okasha says a Selphid in a living body could heal it and Rampage without issue, making Selphids too powerful, and that this would lead to a new Selphid Empire. (Book 10, Ch6.06D L445-447)
- A Selphid in a living host has full control when the host sleeps or is in dire need. (Book 5, Ch1.04D L143)

## Dullahans
- Dullahans have a loose connection between head and body and can take their head off. (Book 2, ChInterlude_B02_1 L179)
- A glow shows inside a Dullahan's armor when the head is off. (Book 2, ChInterlude_B02_1 L179; Book 5, Ch1.02D L242)
- A Dullahan covers flesh and blood with armor, which is effectively skin. (Book 2, ChInterlude_B02_1 L180; Book 3, Ch1.00D L364,L387)
- A Dullahan can detach parts of the armor, including an arm, and the organs beneath are visible. (Book 3, Ch1.00D L362-364)
- Dullahans prefer to cradle their heads in their hands or let them float at their side. (Book 2, ChInterlude_B02_1 L181)
- Dullahans' armor varies in material from wood to metal. (Book 3, Ch1.00D L188)
- Dullahan social rank is set by armor, and the Dullahan in the best armor speaks first. (Book 5, Ch1.03D L283-285)
- Dullahan features change only slightly, and they are private people who do not start conversations with other species. (Book 5, Ch1.03D L216,L280)
- A Dullahan bows his torso and then holds up his head. (Book 4, ChWistram_Days_Pt.7 L220)
- Dullahans are heavy but lighter than Centaurs. (Book 5, Ch1.05D L435)
- A Zombie Dullahan is a nightmare to fight because of the armor. (Book 5, Ch1.03D L85)
- Dullahans live on Baleros and serve in mercenary companies there. (Book 5, Ch1.02D L16,L82)
- A few Dullahans study at Wistram. (Book 4, ChWistram_Days_Pt.3 L255)
- At Pallass, Dullahans hold two seats among hundreds. (Book 11, Ch6.31 L753)

## Half-Elves and Elves
- Half-Elves grow about one year to every three of a Human, and the average half-Elf dies before a hundred. (Book 2, Ch2.00H L9)
- Half-Elves take on the cultures they are raised in, and many do not even like nature. (Book 2, Ch2.19 L213)
- Half-Elves are gifted at magic but do not learn spells faster. (Book 2, ChS03_Wistram_Days_Pt._1 L87)
- {belief} Ceria says rumours claim half-Elves are thieves who cannot be trusted and hate Humans, and that half-Elves have a bad reputation anywhere. (Book 4, ChWistram_Days_Pt.2 L111,L113)
- Ceria tells Pisces that half-Elves once ruled much of Terandria and enslaved Humans, which is a longstanding grudge. (Book 4, ChWistram_Days_Pt.4 L204)
- Half-Elves are more disliked than Stitch-people. (Book 4, ChWistram_Days_Pt.4 L207)
- Half-Elves and Dwarves are known for their distaste for one another. (Book 7, Ch5.07 L84)
- Wistram's Archmage Feor is a half-Elf of two hundred years. (Book 14, Ch6.68 L294)
- Half-Elf bodies are naturally magically inclined. (Book 15, Ch7.02 L263)
- {belief} Ceria's people hold that the Elves are gone, but that magic remains in the blood of those with a drop of Elf blood. (Book 3, Ch3.07H L19,L35)
- In the oldest tales, Elves were immune to charms and curses. (Book 3, Ch3.07H L41)
- {belief} Ceria's great-grandmother says even Dragons bowed to Elves as masters of magic. (Book 3, Ch3.07H L35)
- Both Gnomes and Elves have walked on the twin moons. (Book 2, Ch2.39 L153)
- Elves are not listed in any book of nations, though hints of them can be found. (Book 1, Ch1.07R L313)

## Lizardfolk, Nagas, Lamia and Gorgons
- Lizardfolk live near water, and some can breathe underwater, unlike Drakes who prefer drier climates. (Book 1, Ch1.07 L215)
- Lizardfolk of Baleros are thin and reptilian, with long tails and a colorful neck frill that often contrasts with their scales. (Book 5, Ch1.03D L131)
- A Lizardfolk says they are the biggest species on Baleros, look like Drakes, and are not related to them. (Book 5, Ch1.03D L155)
- Lizardfolk are cautious but quite open to talk with other species. (Book 5, Ch1.03D L280)
- Lizardfolk are cold-blooded, though evolved forms can gain warm blood. (Book 14, ChInterlude_Foliana L402)
- Lizardfolk descend from hunters of Baleros' swamps. (Book 8, ChInterlude_Blackmage L350)
- Most Lizardfolk do not evolve, and most that do become Nagas, Gorgons or Lamia, from which they can change further. (Book 8, ChInterlude_Blackmage L365)
- A Lamia says Gorgons are huge fighters, Nagas are smaller but very strong and the usual form if there is no set path, and Lamia are small with larger brains. (Book 8, ChInterlude_Blackmage L365)
- A Naga has a humanoid torso on a serpentine lower body, with fins on the back of the head like a Lizardperson's frill. (Book 5, Ch1.03D L194)
- Nagas wear clothes on the upper half only, and Lizardfolk admire a flashy Naga. (Book 5, Ch1.03D L197,L202)
- A Star Lamia says her glowing scales store magic, which is very good for spellcasting but makes sneaking around difficult. (Book 8, ChInterlude_Blackmage L362)
- The Lamia Archmage Nailihuaile of Wistram is a Star Lamia whose scales glow with accumulated magic. (Book 13, Ch6.53K L584; Book 14, Ch6.60 L168)
- A Lamia has two humanlike arms and a bipedal upper body with an agile build, unlike the larger Naga or the more imposing Gorgon. (Book 17, ChInterlude_A_Night_in_the_Inn L979)
- A Quexal has feathers, scales and wings, gives up hands, is the fastest of the Lizardfolk evolutions, and is rarer than Lamia or Gorgons. (Book 11, Ch6.20D L697-701)
- Nagas once had a settlement on Izril, and there were bitter wars between Drakes and Lizardfolk. (Book 17, Ch7.25 L788)
- A speaker tells his lord that Lizardfolk are cunning fighters, and their Naga, Gorgon and Medusa forms are powerful and an asset along coasts. (Book 15, Ch7.10K L540)

## Minotaurs
- Minotaurs have a bull's head with horns on a heavily muscled humanoid body, and hooves instead of feet. (Book 10, Ch6.07D L343)
- {belief} Everyone knows Minotaurs are supposed to be honorable, and a Minotaur's word is said to be as good as a Knight's. (Book 1, Ch1.05R L47)
- Ryoka writes that the Minotaurs have an archipelago, feud with other nations, and have tried to take over the world a few times, as almost every species has. (Book 4, Ch3.35 L106)
- The Isles of Minos are a Minotaur kingdom, called the House of Minos by Orthenon and the Empire of Minos in a book Ryoka read. (Book 2, Ch2.31 L417; Book 1, Ch1.07R L310; Book 2, Ch2.00K L264)
- {belief} A book Ryoka read says nearly a million Minotaurs are in a constant state of war and peace against an enemy to the southeast. (Book 1, Ch1.07R L310)
- {belief} A pirate says Minotaurs cannot be beaten with bare hands and that their black ships are deadly at sea and on land. (Book 5, Ch4.17 L216)
- Most Minotaurs do not like to use magic, so few study at Wistram. (Book 4, ChWistram_Days_Pt.3 L278)
- Minotaurs are surprisingly prudish: Venaz refused to bathe with anyone of the opposite sex. (Book 8, ChInterlude_Niers L36)
- Minotaurs love military formations. (Book 8, Ch5.42 L216)
- The House of Minos holds four named holdings, Maweil, Beriad, Hammerad and Samad, according to its marching song. (Book 19, Ch7.47K L237-238)
- The Minotaur song "Home" says they were exiled from every land and found the sands of the House of Minos. (Book 19, Ch7.47K L227-230)
- Everyone in the House of Minos under thirty years of age took part in a competition to study under Niers Astoragon. (Book 11, Ch6.23D L493)
- The House of Minos closed its ports to foreign ships. (Book 2, Ch2.31 L417)
- An emissary says the House of Minos is prepared to go to war against the King of Destruction. (Book 5, Ch4.06KM L406)
- Minotaurs march inland from warships on the dunes in Medain, uninvited by High King Perric, past the Medain-Jecrass battle, saying their foe ahead is the King of Destruction's army. (Book 19, Ch7.46K L1362-1371)

## Centaurs
- Centaurs are half-horse, half-Human, with a Human torso on a horse's body, taller than a horse. (Book 3, Ch1.01D L334; Book 5, Ch1.03D L63)
- Centaurs are heavier than Dullahans. (Book 5, Ch1.05D L435)
- Centaurs dislike small spaces as horses do, and Marian is described as touchy. (Book 2, ChInterlude_B02_1 L200,L254)
- Centaurs hate being ridden by people they do not trust, and might kick a head off. (Book 11, Ch6.21D L659)
- Centaurs have the most complicated childbirths of the species. (Book 10, Ch6.07D L24)
- A Centaur tribe on Baleros found a goldmine in the jungle and fought mercenary companies for it. (Book 3, Ch1.00D L168; Book 3, Ch1.01D L16,L327)
- Centaurs serve as cavalry and run Centaur-led mercenary companies. (Book 2, ChInterlude_B02_1 L201; Book 5, Ch1.02D L82)
- {belief} A character says Centaurs do not kill women unless in battle, even bandits. (Book 10, Ch6.05D L775)
- Baleros mercenary companies recruit Humans, Lizardfolk, Dullahans and Centaurs. (Book 5, Ch1.02D L16)
- A Centaur [Mage] studies at Wistram. (Book 2, ChS03_Wistram_Days_Pt._1 L277,L282)

## Garuda
- Garuda are bird-people covered in feathers, with a beaked face, wings that act as arms ending in talon-like hands, and clawed feet. (Book 7, Ch5.02 L92; Book 8, Ch5.36 L19; Book 8, Ch5.40 L213)
- Garuda live almost exclusively on Chandrar. (Book 8, Ch5.36 L23)
- Garuda are one of the main peoples of Chandrar, with String People and Humans. (Book 5, Ch4.06KM L6)
- Garuda raiders attack armies from the air with the sun behind them. (Book 7, Ch5.02 L67)
- Garuda cannot simply be shot from the sky like hunting fowl, and cannot be chased down. (Book 7, Ch5.02 L53,L152)
- Garuda dislike being called birds and insist they are people, as a bat is not a bird. (Book 9, ChInterlude_Bird L187,L194)
- Garuda in Pallass are few and far between among its Drakes, Gnolls and Dullahans, and they hold no seats on the city council. (Book 11, Ch6.31 L190,L753)
- Bevussa Slenderscale was abandoned as a chick and raised by Drakes in Pallass, which gives her a Drake name. (Book 8, Ch5.36 L23)

## String People
- String People, also called Stitch-People, have bodies of cloth and can reattach limbs, change eyes or redesign themselves given the right materials. (Book 10, Ch6.02 L45; Book 7, Ch5.02 L191; Book 4, ChWistram_Days_Pt.4 L207)
- A String-Girl says she feels sensations like a Human, can change her body within limits, and burns easily. (Book 2, Ch2.12 L109,L114)
- A String Person dies if too much of the core is removed, but over time can replace every part and still be herself. (Book 2, Ch2.12 L118)
- String People need bones and organs, and shaping a body like another creature can go horribly wrong. (Book 2, Ch2.12 L151,L154)
- The fabric matters: cotton is normal, wool or rougher cloth for the poor, canvas makes heavy rough people, and silk would be very costly. (Book 2, Ch2.12 L157-160)
- Skin tone comes from the fabric chosen by the makers. (Book 2, Ch2.12 L122)
- String People are widely accepted, though some people are unsettled by them. (Book 4, ChWistram_Days_Pt.4 L207)
- String People are one of the main peoples of Chandrar, appearing human until the stitches show. (Book 5, Ch4.06KM L6)
- String People are supposed to be fearless warriors, and veiled Stitch-warriors with spears call Flos their King. (Book 5, Ch4.06KM L10-11)
- String People value their own highly. (Book 5, Ch4.19 L319)
- A confederation of String People nations lies to the south of Chandrar. (Book 10, Ch6.12K L31)
- Their home is the Empire of Sands, one of the String People's greatest empires. (Book 7, Ch5.02 L191)

## Demons

- Demons vary in looks: some have wings, talons and beaks, others look nearly normal apart from small differences. (Book 2, Ch1.00C L93-94)
- The war between Humans and Demons has lasted a thousand years or more. (Book 2, Ch1.00C L95)
- A Demon seen by Tom has bright yellow eyes, ram's horns, fur and four toes. (Book 6, Ch1.02C L229)
- Tom thinks Demons are ordinary people with skin conditions who fight as a team. (Book 2, Ch1.01C L82)
- The Demon King uses fearless Demon soldiers who do not fear death. (Book 6, Ch1.02C L240,L247)
- {belief} An unnamed speaker infers that the Demons reached the Blighted King's castle by teleportation magic, a scroll and possibly artifacts, for which the Demon King must have used every magical item he had. (Book 6, Ch1.05C L3,L6-8)
- The Demon King commands powerful Skill users and Deathless. (Book 18, Ch7.34C L409; Book 14, Ch6.68 L44)

- Demon flying forces ambush unprotected patrols, and a [Demon Huntress] warns that patrols need heavy archer or [Mage] cover. (Book 18, Ch7.35C L309-312)

## Dragons
- Dragons are immortal and cannot level. (Book 15, ChInterlude_Queens_and_Dragons L215,L220; Book 1, Ch1.07 L195)
- Dragons have innate magic beyond any [Mage]'s, scales that deflect arrows, and can fly and transform. (Book 15, ChInterlude_Queens_and_Dragons L215)
- Dragons bear few young, and their young age very slowly. (Book 15, ChInterlude_Queens_and_Dragons L216)
- A young Dragon is considered an adult able to leave at two hundred years. (Book 15, ChInterlude_Queens_and_Dragons L215)
- Dragons can sleep for long periods, even a decade at a time. (Book 15, ChInterlude_Queens_and_Dragons L219)
- Dragons have bony spurs for eyebrows, and speak. (Book 2, Ch2.39 L95; Book 15, Ch7.00 L21)
- Ryoka, observing Teriarch smile, thinks Dragons are conceited, prideful and not a little susceptible to flattery. (Book 2, Ch2.39 L117)
- Dragons are greedy and possessive about their hoards. (Book 2, Ch2.39 L251; Book 9, Ch5.50G L5)
- Dragons are touchy and dislike losing. (Book 2, ChInterlude_B02_1 L261)
- Dragons are formal and mark milestones such as a two hundredth year. (Book 2, Ch2.25 L381)
- Dragons sustain flight with magic. (Book 5, Ch4.16 L85)
- Drakes are distant cousins and descendants of Dragons. (Book 1, Ch1.07 L112,L217; Book 7, Ch5.18S L103)
- The Walled Cities were constructed in the age of Dragons. (Book 6, ChS02_The_Antinium_Wars_Pt.3 L202)

- Dragons are almost extinct, and one is slain only every few thousand years. (Book 4, Ch3.35 L110)
- Only two Dragons remain on Izril, one of them a young woman raised in Manus. (Book 15, ChInterlude_Queens_and_Dragons L218,L119)
- Wyverns are inbred cousins of Dragons that, unlike Dragons, lack speech and vast intelligence. (Book 15, Ch7.00 L21)

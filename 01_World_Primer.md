## Premise: Erin and the inn
- Erin Solstice is a twenty-year-old woman from Michigan who likes strategy games such as chess, shogi and go. (Book 1, Ch1.02 L177)
- Erin reaches this world by accident: she only wanted to go to the bathroom and instead found herself looking down the snout of a dragon. (Book 1, Ch1.01 L9-10)
- {event} In the first chapter a burned and bleeding Erin finds a long-deserted inn built of oak boards on a stone foundation, about four times the size of a normal house. (Book 1, Ch1.00 L4-6,L12)
- {event} Cleaning the inn's common room gives Erin the [Innkeeper] class at Level 1 with the Skills Basic Cleaning and Basic Cooking. (Book 1, Ch1.01 L86-89)
- Erin concludes that the world works like a video game, with levels and Skills announced as thoughts in her head. (Book 1, Ch1.02 L11-15)
- The inn stands on a long, gradual incline of the grassland, and Liscor can be seen in the distance from it. (Book 1, Ch1.12 L44-49)
- Erin estimates the walk from the inn to Liscor at about an hour, although Relc and Klbkch had called it ten minutes. (Book 1, Ch1.12 L46-49)
- The inn was once part of a small community that died of a plague, and no law forbids occupying the site. (Book 1, Ch1.07 L141,L149)
- The first people to visit are Relc, a Drake, and Klbkch, an Antinium, two Liscor guardsmen sent to check on smoke seen from the city walls. (Book 1, Ch1.07 L71-72)
- {event} Erin paints the new name "The Wandering Inn" on the inn's plaque and hammers a sign into the ground beside it reading "No Killing Goblins". (Book 1, Ch1.20 L217-219)
- Goblins are a constant threat around the inn from the first days, and Erin's refusal to see them as targets becomes the first thread of the story. (Book 1, Ch1.03 L5-13; Book 1, Ch1.17 L85-103)
- While Erin sleeps in her inn, a ritual far away summons a crowd of young Humans from Earth, who appear mid-motion holding smartphones and scatter across the world. (Book 1, ChInterlude_B01_1 L7-12)
- The summoned Humans are not legends and have no special powers beyond those all Humans have. (Book 1, ChInterlude_B01_1 L46)
- Ryoka Griffin, a barefoot Runner who knows she is in another world with no way back, is introduced as a second Earth arrival. (Book 1, ChInterlude_1.00_R L89-91)
- Chess is the first thing that ties Erin to the city: she wins a wager against the Drake Olesm and so recovers money she was overcharged. (Book 1, Ch1.19 L63-68; Book 1, Ch1.20 L9)
- The Antinium Queen allows twelve Workers to visit the inn, where Erin teaches them and a Goblin group to play chess. (Book 1, Ch1.24 L35-52,L277-306)

## The world at a glance: continents
- Ryoka lists the continents as Baleros, the Blighted Kingdoms of Rhir, Terandria, Chandrar, and Izril. (Book 2, Ch2.08 L14-19)
- Ryoka's reading confirms five main continents - Rhir, Izril, Terandria, Chandrar and Baleros - and that Humans live on all of them in some form. (Book 4, Ch3.35 L104)
- Baleros is described as a mysterious continent of jungles and Gazers. (Book 2, Ch2.08 L15)
- The Blighted Kingdoms of Rhir are described as dying lands locked in eternal conflict. (Book 2, Ch2.08 L16)
- Terandria is described as a kingdom-continent of knights, royalty and Humans. (Book 2, Ch2.08 L17)
- Chandrar is the home of the King of Destruction and holds a desert larger than the United States. (Book 2, Ch2.08 L18)
- The Drakes call the home continent Issrysil, while Ryoka prefers the name Izril. (Book 2, Ch2.08 L19)
- Selys, drawing a map for Erin, calls the continent "Semariss" and says Humans have another name for it. (Book 1, Ch1.28 L223)
- Ryoka's reading shows a world far larger than Earth, where nations the size of the United States or Russia count as normal or small. (Book 4, Ch3.35 L78)
- {belief} Ryoka concludes that war dominates the history she reads, and that large empires fail because of supply, death of the ruler, rising high-level individuals and dependence on magic. (Book 4, Ch3.35 L74-84)
- Other peoples named in Ryoka's reading as powers: the Slave Traders of Roshal on Chandrar, about as powerful as Wistram but neutral. (Book 4, Ch3.35 L105)
- The Minotaurs hold an archipelago and have tried to take over the world several times. (Book 4, Ch3.35 L106)

## Izril: the home continent
- Izril's Humans live across the northern part of the continent, and a long line of mountains cuts off the northern third from the south. (Book 1, Ch1.28 L225-226)
- Liscor sits near the middle where the continent narrows, and is one of the few ways through the mountains besides the High Passes and the coastal routes. (Book 1, Ch1.28 L227-229)
- The High Passes are unsafe even for high-level adventurers because of nasty creatures living there. (Book 1, Ch1.28 L229-231)
- The south of Izril is home to Drakes, Gnolls and Antinium, with a few other races. (Book 1, Ch1.28 L232-233)
- Selys marks six known Antinium colonies in the south; the ones other than Liscor's are described as hostile. (Book 1, Ch1.28 L235-237)
- The big southern groupings are the Walled Cities, the Gnoll Tribes of the plains, and the independent ports and cities. (Book 1, Ch1.28 L243)
- Nearly a third of the southern population are Harpies and Naga according to Selys, with some Minotaurs coming and going. (Book 1, Ch1.28 L243)
- The southern cities fight constantly, and Liscor's army is usually hired out on campaign elsewhere. (Book 1, Ch1.23 L172; Book 1, Ch1.28 L239-241)
- There are six Walled Cities, and Pallass is the northernmost of them. (Book 5, Ch4.16 L7)
- The Walled Cities named in a joint call during the Face-Eater Moth crisis are Zeres, Pallass, Salazsar, Manus, Fissival and Oteslia. (Book 7, Ch5.08 L258-266)
- The Walled Cities differ in government: some elect an official, some are run by a body of powerful individuals and some by a hereditary leader. (Book 2, ChS02_The_Antinium_Wars_Pt.1 L37)
- {belief} A history book claims the Walled Cities were built to defend against Dragons, and that the Drakes set the bones of their Dragon ancestors into the walls. (Book 6, ChS02_The_Antinium_Wars_Pt.3 L207,L212)
- A Walled City's ruling nobles are called Wall Lords or Lords of the Wall, as in Wall Lord Ilvriss. (Book 2, Ch2.24 L252,L276)
- The Five Families of the human north - Reinhart, Veltras, Terland, Wellfar and El - were the first Humans to settle on Izril, are related to Terandrian kings, and remain rich and influential. (Book 1, Ch1.41 L186)
- First Landing is the largest Human city on Izril, a port on the north sea built by Terandrian nobility who fled, and the Reinhart mansion stands there. (Book 5, Ch4.26M L45-47)
- Lady Magnolia Reinhart is the heir to the Reinhart family and one of the richest people on the continent. (Book 2, Ch2.30 L80)
- Invrisil, the city of adventurers, is a large Human city about six hundred miles north of Celum. (Book 2, Ch2.09 L237,L240)
- Riverfarm is a village with no ruler but its village head, in the early days. (Book 3, Ch3.00E L91)
- Liscor's Hive, the one Antinium colony inside a southern city, exists under a bargain with the southern cities. (Book 1, Ch1.28 L237)
- Liscor's guard treats Goblins as monsters and bandits when judging crime. (Book 1, Ch1.17 L93)
- {belief} Krshia says Wistram is no friend to the magicless and that Gnolls will not deal with it, a grudge dating from a gathering of the Gnoll tribes nearly twenty years earlier. (Book 1, Ch1.35 L118,L123,L131)

## Liscor at a glance
- Liscor is a Drake-run city with Gnolls and a single Antinium Hive, and Humans are rare there, about one for every forty nonhumans in Erin's early days. (Book 1, Ch1.28 L227-229,L237; Book 1, Ch1.30 L201)
- Drakes and Gnolls are openly hostile to each other in Liscor's Adventurer's Guild in Erin's first visit. (Book 1, Ch1.13 L72-73)
- Liscor's army is usually hired out on campaign elsewhere, and a saying holds that rain, the undead and war are the three most dangerous things to Liscor. (Book 1, Ch1.23 L139,L172)
- Watch Captain Zevara leads Liscor's City Watch. (Book 1, Ch1.42 L185,L194)
- Under Klbkch's account of the agreement with Liscor, Antinium Soldiers may go aboveground only at the request of a figure of authority or in war or the defence of the city. (Book 2, Ch2.03 L89)
- Xrn tells Klbkch that in over two decades since the Antinium reached Izril, no new Queens have been born. (Book 5, Ch4.07 L121)

## Terandria
- Terandria is the continent of Human kingdoms that sent nobles fleeing to found First Landing on Izril. (Book 5, Ch4.26M L45)
- Ryoka's reading places the Dwarves on Terandria, although they once lived everywhere. (Book 4, Ch3.35 L108)
- Lyonette gives her family as the house of Marquin, one of the Hundred Families of Terandria, and sixth in line to the Eternal Throne of Calanfer. (Book 2, Ch2.21 L7)
- Calanfer's throne is called eternal but dates back about six thousand years, according to Lyonette. (Book 10, Ch6.03 L235)
- In the north of Terandria the nation of Ailendamus is described as thrice the size of Calanfer, Gaiil-Drome and Kaliv put together. (Book 12, Ch6.44E L241)
- Calanfer, Gaiil-Drome and Kaliv held off Ailendamus and six other nations in the last war, Petril's Folly, twenty-eight years earlier. (Book 12, Ch6.44E L242)
- Kaliv is a high-altitude nation of Humans whose rulers are Griffin Kings or Queens. (Book 12, Ch6.44E L240,L243)

## Baleros
- On Baleros much of the fighting is done by mercenary groups, from small troops to huge Companies, and the Companies have their own cities and act like nations. (Book 3, Ch1.00D L172-175)
- The Four Great Companies are the biggest, and a soldier says that if two fought, the other two would attack the winner. (Book 3, Ch1.00D L177-180)
- One of the Four Great Companies is the Armored Legion, which is largely Dullahan and based mainly in the north. (Book 3, Ch1.00D L183-184)
- Niers Astoragon is second in command of one of the Four Great Companies and the highest-leveled [Strategist] on the continent, nicknamed the Titan. (Book 2, Ch2.17 L359; Book 2, ChInterlude_B02_1 L156-157)
- Niers is a Fraerling, a people about as tall as a Human's hand who are reclusive on Baleros. (Book 2, ChInterlude_B02_1 L157-159)

## Chandrar
- Chandrar is an arid continent, with heat by day and cold by night in many regions, and a vast desert at its centre that cuts off civilization for thousands of miles. (Book 10, Ch6.12K L25,L27)
- The nations of Chandrar border the great central desert rather than lying within it. (Book 10, Ch6.12K L27; Book 10, Ch6.14K L422)
- Reim, home of Flos, lies in the northeast of Chandrar and borders the great desert, which makes it dry. (Book 10, Ch6.14K L422)
- Germina is one of four remaining Shield Kingdoms, its capital Ger sits on an oasis, and its ruler bears the title Quarass. (Book 10, Ch6.12K L29)
- Hellios, northeast of Germina, was ruled by Queen Calliope after Flos killed her husband in his early wars. (Book 10, Ch6.12K L30)
- To the north lie the Republic of Belchan, which elects a [Prime Minister], and the Realm of Jecrass, which has a [King]. (Book 10, Ch6.12K L32)
- West of Reim lies Khelt, a land whose army is undead, led by King Fetohep. (Book 10, Ch6.14K L449,L460-463)
- The Empire of Sands is the newest power on Chandrar, claiming the western civilizations across the great desert under a new [Emperor], and is hostile to Flos. (Book 10, Ch6.12K L34)
- South of Reim lies Nerrhavia's Fall, a once-great empire of String People overthrown by the kingdom that now bears the name. (Book 10, Ch6.14K L488-490)
- Tiqr, a Garuda-populated land with war elephants, is the one nation Flos expects might ally with him. (Book 10, Ch6.14K L483)
- Pomle, Savere and Illivere are smaller states Flos names as possible threats. (Book 10, Ch6.14K L493)
- The Thalassocracy of Medain is a nation of adventurers with three vast magical dungeons. (Book 10, Ch6.14K L498)
- Flos conquered nearly all of Chandrar and landed armies on Izril's southern shores before he vanished. (Book 2, Ch2.10 L124)
- The Slave Traders of Roshal keep neutral and buy and sell captives, including those taken in Flos's war on Reim. (Book 5, Ch4.06KM L18,L66)

## Rhir
- Rhir is a continent under constant war, ruled by the Blighted King, who fights monster tribes and demons. (Book 1, Ch1.07R L305)
- The heart of the kingdom is the shield city of Paranfer, whose four great walls have stood for thousands of years. (Book 6, Ch1.02C L41)
- Rhir is at war with a Demon King, leader of the continent's Demons and monsters. (Book 6, Ch1.02C L175)
- The Antinium came from Rhir, arriving on Izril in four small ships in the last month of winter. (Book 2, ChS02_The_Antinium_Wars_Pt.1 L17)
- Rhir has no Assassin's Guild, since infighting of that kind would be too harmful. (Book 6, Ch1.04C L70)
- Ryoka's reading finds that Rhir keeps producing new horrors and the Demons emerged five thousand years back. (Book 4, Ch3.35 L90-94)

## Wistram
- Wistram is the Isle of Mages, where at least three archmages have their homes. (Book 1, Ch1.35 L105)
- New Wistram students are taken once a year; a month costs twenty gold and an exam decides who may stay free. (Book 4, ChWistram_Days_Pt.1 L7,L9,L178-180)
- Wistram's mages broadcast and amplify scrying across the world during crises. (Book 7, Ch5.08 L268)

## The main peoples at a glance
- Humans live on every continent, and on Izril they hold the north above the mountains. (Book 4, Ch3.35 L104; Book 1, Ch1.28 L225-226)
- Drakes are related to dragons, breathe fire, and are insulted when mistaken for lizardfolk. (Book 1, Ch1.07 L112,L215-219)
- Drakes hold the southern cities of Izril, including Liscor and the Walled Cities. (Book 1, Ch1.28 L232-233,L243)
- Gnolls live in tribes on the plains of southern Izril, and the tribes gather to talk and form alliances every ten years. (Book 1, Ch1.28 L243; Book 1, Ch1.35 L131)
- Beastkin come from another continent further south and are mostly found among the Gnoll tribes. (Book 1, Ch1.28 L245)
- The Antinium arrived from Rhir as a new species, and Liscor's Hive is a bargain-based colony inside a southern city. (Book 2, ChS02_The_Antinium_Wars_Pt.1 L14,L17; Book 1, Ch1.28 L237)
- Antinium Workers can think as a hive mind: a hundred play chess as a single mind. (Book 1, Ch1.27 L151-153)
- Goblins are small, wiry, with pointed ears, two rows of shark-like teeth and blood-red eyes, and they scream as they chase. (Book 1, Ch1.03 L5-13)
- Goblins are said to always return however often they are wiped out, and a Goblin King's rise is feared. (Book 4, Ch3.35 L111)
- Half-Elves keep the name though no one has seen an Elf for thousands of years. (Book 1, Ch1.37 L103,L106)
- Dullahans are covered in armor except for the head, and the quality of the armor varies. (Book 3, Ch1.00D L188)
- Selphids are body-stealing "parasites" who live by inhabiting bodies. (Book 2, Ch2.22 L419-422)
- The Garuda are bird-people native to Chandrar. (Book 5, Ch4.00K L35)
- Fraerlings, or the Small Folk, are a Baleros people about as tall as a Human's hand. (Book 2, ChInterlude_B02_1 L159)
- Minotaurs appear as adventurers on Izril, and they also hold an archipelago. (Book 1, Ch1.02R L4; Book 4, Ch3.35 L106)
- The String People of Chandrar once formed a single empire that unified about six thousand years earlier. (Book 10, Ch6.14K L488)
- Dragons are mostly dead in Ryoka's reading, and Drakes call them ancestors. (Book 4, Ch3.35 L110; Book 1, Ch1.07 L217)
- Frost Faeries are treated by Liscor's people as a force of nature rather than as creatures. (Book 2, Ch2.10 L109)

## Everyday life: money
- Coins in common use are gold, silver, copper and bronze. (Book 1, Ch1.14 L4; Book 1, Ch1.09 L45)
- A silver coin is ten copper and a gold coin is twenty silver. (Book 11, Ch6.26 L323)
- The average worker earns about three or four silver coins a day. (Book 1, Ch1.01R L143)
- One gold coin is a lot for villagers; every family might have one gold coin's worth in savings. (Book 3, Ch3.11E L285)
- Erin's first purchase, a bag of flour, oil, butter, four sausages, two onions and sugar, salt and yeast, is priced at three gold and eight silver. (Book 1, Ch1.13 L165)
- A bathhouse in Liscor costs five copper for Drakes, with Gnolls and other furred Beastkin paying double. (Book 1, Ch1.22 L47)

## Everyday life: calendar and seasons
- Years are counted in A.F.; Durene, a villager of Riverfarm, thinks the current year is around 22 A.F. (Book 3, Ch3.00E L90-91)
- The book on the Second Antinium War opens in the year 13 A.F., with Izril tentatively at peace. (Book 6, ChS02_The_Antinium_Wars_Pt.3 L13)
- Ryoka finds that many old books date events by an event-based system rather than a numbered calendar. (Book 4, Ch3.35 L88)
- Rhir has its own calendar, with years named in numbers and names such as 2118 Zekol. (Book 14, Ch6.68 L33)
- The first Antinium landed on Izril in the last month of winter. (Book 2, ChS02_The_Antinium_Wars_Pt.1 L17)
- The Antinium of Liscor's Hive do not go aboveground in the first two months of spring. (Book 7, Ch5.03 L268)
- The Winter Solstice is the shortest day of the year, and the Frost Faeries say it marks the end of two thirds of winter. (Book 4, ChInterlude_B04_1 L40-43)
- The Winter Solstice stands as close to a new year as this world has, in Erin's view. (Book 4, Ch3.39 L457)
- Cities celebrate special days with festivals offering candied apples, games and city-bought entertainments; the Winter Solstice festival in Celum is a romantic time. (Book 3, Ch3.15 L238-243)
- A sand hourglass is used to time the twenty minutes until dinner in an Earther company's camp. (Book 11, Ch6.20D L261-262)

## Everyday life: travel and communication
- Runners deliver quickly, and merchants, caravans and ship captains also carry letters between cities and continents for a few coppers. (Book 1, Ch1.31 L147,L153)
- Letters change hands like currency, so a letter to another continent can cost a copper coin or two and take a month or two. (Book 1, Ch1.31 L151-153)
- Couriers are rare long-distance specialists, fewer than a hundred on the continent, running around a thousand miles on a delivery. (Book 2, Ch2.13 L167-169)
- Adventurers send [Message] spells through the local Mage's Guild, which hands the request to the Runner's Guild for delivery. (Book 3, Ch3.02H L56)
- A Runner collects a seal as proof of a delivery, and Lady Magnolia's own seal is half silver and half translucent sapphire-blue stone. (Book 1, ChInterlude_1.00_R L48,L55)
- Runner's Guilds in the north use bags of holding, so a Runner can reach a village thirty miles away within the hour. (Book 4, Ch3.37 L157-161)
- Between Walled Cities, emergency calls run through [Message] and [Scrying], with Wistram able to amplify visual spells. (Book 7, Ch5.08 L242,L268)
- Erin's enchanted door can be paired with a second door or anchor stone to open a portal between two places, such as Celum and Liscor. (Book 4, Ch3.30 L159)
- The door drinks a great deal of mana, and once spent takes a long time to recharge. (Book 4, Ch3.30 L160-161)
- A test by Pisces and Moore finds the door can send a person about 536 miles and then needs roughly 8.2 hours to recharge. (Book 5, Ch4.12 L457-459)
- First Landing is the largest port on the human side of Izril and lies close to four thousand miles north of Celum. (Book 2, Ch2.09 L66,L262)
- Television arrives in the world as magicvision, a new technology colored by influence from Earth. (Book 15, Ch7.10K L89-90)
- The Unmarked Coach is a hard-to-request, fast transport counterpart to Magnolia Reinhart's pink carriage. (Book 18, ChInterlude_Carriages_and_Conversations L47)

## The big institutions at a glance
- Adventurer's Guilds rank teams Bronze, Silver and Gold, and beyond Gold the strongest become Named adventurers known across their region. (Book 1, Ch1.11R L32-35)
- A Gold-rank team can match a small army, while a Bronze-rank team is not much better than rookies. (Book 1, Ch1.11R L32-33)
- The Runner's Guild employs City Runners, who use bags of holding in the north, while Couriers handle long hauls. (Book 4, Ch3.37 L157-161; Book 2, Ch2.13 L167)
- The Merchant's Guild handles mundane traffic by caravan and is a rival to the Runner's Guild, slower but cheaper for goods with protection. (Book 3, Ch3.24 L13; Book 4, Ch3.37 L164)
- The Mage's Guild carries [Message] spells and told Erin it would not allow her door to take over messages between Liscor and Celum. (Book 3, Ch3.02H L56; Book 4, Ch3.30 L156)
- {belief} A speaker warns Ryoka that the Assassin's Guild in Izril is quite competent, and calls Liscor's army its de facto governing body. (Book 5, Ch4.24 L295)
- Guilds are powerful in cities but Lords hold their own standing forces, and Guilds pick their battles rarely, hiring mercenaries or fighting as the Adventurer's and Runner's Guilds do. (Book 4, Ch3.37 L144)
- {belief} Reynold says even a [Shopkeeper] can sell a [Lord], meaning merchants have political influence. (Book 4, Ch3.37 L146)
- The Five Families maneuver in the north's politics, and Drakes in the south play a more direct version. (Book 4, Ch3.37 L151)
- Wistram claims to be nationless and a nation unto itself, never bowing to a ruler. (Book 1, Ch1.35 L129)
- The Four Great Companies of Baleros are mercenary Companies that act like nations. (Book 3, Ch1.00D L172-180)
- The Slave Traders of Roshal are a neutral power on Chandrar about as strong as Wistram. (Book 4, Ch3.35 L105)
